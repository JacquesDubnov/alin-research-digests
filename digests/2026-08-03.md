### Psychological Symptoms During Perimenopause: A Global Perspective
- **Authors:** Cunningham et al. (Flo Health / Mayo Clinic)
- **Journal:** Menopause, 2026 Jul 28
- **PMID:** 42517353
- **DOI:** 10.1097/GME.0000000000002872
- **Domain:** Query 2 — perimenopause + psychological symptoms
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Large international study (n=7,975 Flo app users across 20 countries) showing perimenopause MRS psychological subscale scores were significantly higher than premenopause (7.3 vs 4.8); exhaustion was the biggest differentiator — high-impact evidence underpinning ALIN's focus on perimenopausal cognitive and emotional burden.

> "Across all geographic regions, perimenopausal women reported significantly greater psychological symptom burden on the Menopause Rating Scale than premenopausal peers. Exhaustion, depressive mood, and irritability showed the steepest perimenopausal elevation. The consistency of findings across 20 countries suggests a universal biological underpinning."

### Modeling Cardiovascular Risk Across the Menopause Transition: A Synthetic Cohort Study
- **Authors:** Vallée A.
- **Journal:** Menopause, 2026 Jul 28
- **PMID:** 42517354
- **DOI:** 10.1097/GME.0000000000002851
- **Domain:** Query 4 — menopause + cardiovascular risk
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Synthetic cohort (100,000 women) quantifying the menopause-attributable rise in ASCVD risk: 10-year risk climbs from 1.18% premenopausal to 4.35% postmenopausal — vivid modelling data for ALIN's cardiovascular health communications.

> "Using a validated microsimulation framework, 10-year ASCVD risk in the synthetic cohort increased from 1.18% in the premenopausal phase to 4.35% in the postmenopausal phase, with the steepest inflection during the early menopausal transition. Modifiable risk factor control attenuated but did not eliminate the menopause-attributable excess."

### The Integration of Migraine and Sleep Disturbance in Perimenopause: A Narrative Review
- **Authors:** Heale R, Tait C.
- **Journal:** Menopause, 2026 Jul 28
- **PMID:** 42517357
- **DOI:** 10.1097/GME.0000000000002863
- **Domain:** Query 2 — perimenopause + sleep/neurological
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Narrative review examining the bidirectional relationship between migraine and sleep disruption in perimenopause, with hormone-fluctuation mechanisms and evidence-based management recommendations — actionable content for ALIN's sleep and headache modules.

> "Migraine and sleep disturbance co-occur at elevated rates during perimenopause. Estrogen fluctuations lower the migraine threshold while disrupting circadian rhythm and sleep architecture; conversely, sleep deprivation sensitises trigeminal pain pathways. An integrated management approach targeting both conditions simultaneously may offer greater symptom relief than single-domain treatment."

### Elinzanetant in Postmenopausal Women With Sleep Disturbance: Results From the Randomized, Phase II NIRVANA Study
- **Authors:** Baker et al.
- **Journal:** Sleep, 2026 Jul 28
- **PMID:** 42520241
- **DOI:** 10.1093/sleep/zsag208
- **Domain:** Query 3 — NK3 antagonist + menopause
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Phase II RCT (n=110) showing elinzanetant significantly reduced polysomnography-measured wake after sleep onset (WASO) at week 4 (LS-mean difference vs placebo: −22.50 min) in postmenopausal women with sleep disturbance — extends the elinzanetant evidence base into objective sleep outcomes beyond vasomotor symptoms.

> "At week 4, elinzanetant significantly reduced PSG-measured WASO versus placebo (LS-mean difference: −22.50 min; 95% CI −38.60 to −6.40; p=0.0063). Subjective sleep quality assessed by PSQI also improved. The safety profile was consistent with previous elinzanetant trials; no new safety signals were identified."

### VEMORA: Vaginal Estrogen versus Non-Hormonal Moisturiser in Women Receiving Aromatase Inhibitors — A Randomized, Controlled Trial
- **Authors:** Niravath et al.
- **Journal:** Breast Cancer Research and Treatment, 2026 Jul 30
- **PMID:** 42530772
- **DOI:** 10.1007/s10549-026-08025-0
- **Domain:** Query 5 — vaginal estrogen + menopause/oncology
- **Relevance to ALIN:** First RCT (n=23) directly comparing low-dose vaginal estrogen vs. Replens moisturiser in breast cancer patients on aromatase inhibitors; VE significantly improved vaginal dryness (p=0.049) and dyspareunia (p=0.048) with serum estradiol remaining within postmenopausal range — directly informs ALIN's GSM content for women with breast cancer concerns.

> "Vaginal estrogen was associated with significant improvement in vaginal dryness (p=0.049) and dyspareunia (p=0.048) compared with Replens over 12 weeks. Serum estradiol levels remained within the normal postmenopausal range in all participants receiving vaginal estrogen, providing reassurance about systemic absorption in women on aromatase inhibitors."

### Perceived Stress as a Mediator Between Negative Life Events and Psychosocial Quality of Life During the Menopausal Transition: A Path Analysis
- **Authors:** Kuck et al.
- **Journal:** Comprehensive Psychoneuroendocrinology, 2026 Jul 22
- **PMID:** 42540445
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.cpnec.2026.100366
- **Domain:** Query 2 — perimenopause + psychosocial
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Path analysis (n=269) demonstrating that perceived stress explained 47% of variance in psychosocial quality of life during the menopausal transition, mediating the impact of negative life events — strong empirical support for ALIN's stress-management and resilience-building content.

> "Perceived stress fully mediated the relationship between negative life events and psychosocial QoL (β=−0.47; p<0.001). The total indirect effect accounted for 47% of variance in psychosocial QoL. Interventions targeting stress appraisal and coping during the menopausal transition may be particularly effective."

### Can Menopausal Status Moderate the Effects of Aerobic Exercise on Executive Functions? Evidence From a Randomized Controlled Trial
- **Authors:** Sanz Simon et al.
- **Journal:** Journal of Alzheimer's Disease, 2026 Aug 1
- **PMID:** 42541423
- **DOI:** 10.1177/13872877261471900
- **Domain:** Query 4 — menopause + cognitive function
- **Relevance to ALIN:** RCT (n=93) finding postmenopausal women showed significantly greater improvement in executive function from aerobic exercise versus stretching, compared to perimenopausal women — supports ALIN's targeted exercise recommendations for cognitive preservation in postmenopause.

> "Postmenopausal women assigned to aerobic exercise demonstrated significantly greater gains in composite executive function scores compared to the stretching control group (d=0.72; p=0.003). Perimenopausal women showed a non-significant trend. Menopausal status moderated exercise response, suggesting a window of opportunity for cognitive benefit from aerobic training."
