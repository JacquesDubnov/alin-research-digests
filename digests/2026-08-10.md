### Management of Menopausal Hormone Therapy
- **Authors:** Switzer J et al.
- **Journal:** Current Opinion in Obstetrics & Gynecology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42563637
- **DOI:** [10.1097/GCO.0000000000001135](https://doi.org/10.1097/GCO.0000000000001135)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/Menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Practical clinical review covering personalized MHT risk assessment, timing hypothesis for CVD and cognition, and the VTE-safety advantage of transdermal estradiol + micronized progesterone — directly maps to ALIN's safety and treatment modules.

> Reanalysis of the WHI and recent studies show a protective role of estrogen in CVD and bone health when initiated before 60. Transdermal estradiol + micronized progesterone does not appear to increase VTE risk. The timing hypothesis is applicable to both CVD and cognition.

### Integrative Medicine Approaches for Menopause
- **Authors:** Patel J et al.
- **Journal:** Obstetrics and Gynecology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42561418
- **DOI:** [10.1097/AOG.0000000000006388](https://doi.org/10.1097/AOG.0000000000006388)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/Menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Comprehensive evidence-based narrative review of non-hormonal options — CBT, clinical hypnosis, mindfulness, acupuncture, botanicals — organized by strength of evidence. Key resource for ALIN's non-hormonal symptom guidance.

> CBT and clinical hypnosis significantly reduce vasomotor symptom distress and are recommended as first-line non-hormonal options. Mindfulness may improve sleep and QoL but is not recommended for VMS. Phytoestrogens and botanicals show inconsistent high-quality evidence and are not advised for routine use.

### Real-World Safety Profile of Fezolinetant: A Disproportionality Analysis Based on the FAERS Database
- **Authors:** Wei S et al.
- **Journal:** Naunyn-Schmiedeberg's Archives of Pharmacology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42547587
- **DOI:** [10.1007/s00210-026-05773-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00210-026-05773-0)
- **Domain:** Query 3 (Non-hormonal / NK3 antagonists)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Critical pharmacovigilance data for fezolinetant (Veozah). Identifies a broader cholestatic liver injury phenotype and "nightmare" as a potential new signal in real-world use beyond what clinical trials captured.

> Analysis of 1,757 FAERS cases found 20 positive AE signals meeting all four algorithms, with liver-related laboratory abnormalities (ALT/AST/GGT/bilirubin) prominent. Median AE onset was 38 days; most events within first 100 days. Nightmare was flagged as a potential novel signal. Post-2024 reports showed stronger hepatic signals, possibly reflecting stimulated reporting after FDA communications.

- **PMID:** 42566226
- **DOI:** [10.1080/13697137.2026.2702818](https://doi.org/10.1080/13697137.2026.2702818)
- **Domain:** Query 5 (Vaginal estrogen / Genitourinary syndrome)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** RCT of a non-hormonal, non-drug GSM treatment (photobiomodulation + pelvic floor training) with significant improvement in sexual function scores — adds to ALIN's portfolio of evidence-based non-pharmacological options.

### Fractional Carbon Dioxide Vaginal Laser for the Treatment of Urinary Symptoms: 36-Month Results
- **Authors:** Zwaans BMM et al.
- **Journal:** International Urology and Nephrology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42552278
- **DOI:** [10.1007/s11255-026-05314-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11255-026-05314-x)
- **Domain:** Query 5 (Vaginal estrogen / Genitourinary syndrome)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Longest follow-up (36 months) data on CO2 vaginal laser for GSM, showing durable improvements in OAB/urgency symptoms and vulvovaginal dryness — important context for ALIN users considering energy-based device treatments.

> In 73 menopausal women (mean age 55), 44.7% rated themselves "markedly or moderately better" at 36 months. OAB symptoms and quality-of-life scores significantly improved at each follow-up. Vulvar dryness (p=0.0016) and burning (p=0.0086) significantly reduced. SUI leaks were not altered but QUID-SUI scores significantly improved.

### Menopause Management in Patients With Cancer
- **Authors:** Ward KK et al.
- **Journal:** Obstetrics and Gynecology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42561416
- **DOI:** [10.1097/AOG.0000000000006393](https://doi.org/10.1097/AOG.0000000000006393)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (Cardiovascular / Breast cancer)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Practical symptom-based framework for menopause management in cancer survivors — critical for ALIN users who are cancer survivors for whom standard HRT guidance does not apply.

> Non-hormonal therapies (SNRIs, gabapentin, lifestyle) are first-line across cancer types. Vaginal estrogen is appropriate for most gynecologic cancers and can be considered in select breast cancer survivors. Systemic HRT remains contraindicated in hormone receptor-positive breast cancer but may be considered in non-hormone-dependent malignancies. Thrombotic risk, hepatic function, and drug interactions must be factored in.

### Premature Menopause and Hypertension as Combined Risk Factors for Cardiovascular Events in Women: A Nationwide Cohort Analysis
- **Authors:** Choi KU et al.
- **Journal:** Menopause, 2026
- **PMID:** 42549666
- **DOI:** [10.1097/GME.0000000000002867](https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000002867)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (Cardiovascular)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Large nationwide cohort (1.17M women) quantifying the combined CVD hazard of premature menopause + hypertension — actionable data for ALIN's CVD risk education and early screening messaging.

> Women with both premature menopause (before 40) and hypertension had an adjusted HR of 1.64 for CVD events vs 1.42 for hypertension alone and 1.18 for premature menopause alone. Combined risk translated to 18.4 CVD events per 1,000 person-years vs 6.0 in those with neither risk factor.

### Inflammatory and Immune Biomarkers Over Midlife and Future Cognition in Women: the SWAN HDL Ancillary Study
- **Authors:** Qi M et al.
- **Journal:** Menopause, 2026
- **PMID:** 42549661
- **DOI:** [10.1097/GME.0000000000002874](https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000002874)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (Cognitive)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** SWAN prospective data linking midlife systemic inflammation (GlycA) to worse future working memory — supports ALIN's messaging on anti-inflammatory lifestyle strategies for cognitive preservation around menopause.

> Higher midlife GlycA was associated with significantly worse working memory over the following decade. Higher baseline complement factor 4 and increases in C3/C4 were associated with better recall, suggesting a protective immune role. Targeting inflammation during the menopause transition may be a modifiable avenue for cognitive health.

- **DOI:** [10.1097/GME.0000000000002871](https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000002871)

- **Relevance to ALIN:** RCT demonstrating a significant improvement in menopause symptom burden (MRS-II) and quality of life with a specific probiotic formula — adds emerging evidence for microbiome-based interventions in ALIN's non-hormonal toolkit.

### Empagliflozin Improves Endothelial Function in an Ovariectomized Rat Model of Estrogen Deficiency
- **Authors:** Mad Azli AA et al.
- **Journal:** Journal of Cardiovascular Translational Research, 2026
- **PMID:** 42563069
- **DOI:** [10.1007/s12265-026-10823-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12265-026-10823-2)
- **Domain:** Queries 1, 3, 4 (Non-hormonal / Cardiovascular)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Preclinical evidence that SGLT2 inhibitors (already used for T2D/heart failure) restore endothelial function in estrogen deficiency conditions — relevant context as many ALIN users may be on these medications or exploring non-hormonal cardiovascular strategies.

> In ovariectomized rats, empagliflozin significantly improved vascular reactivity, enhanced eNOS activation, and restored nitric oxide levels, with effects comparable to estradiol. Identifies SGLT2 inhibitors as a potential non-hormonal therapeutic strategy for postmenopausal endothelial dysfunction.

- **Authors:** Yasui Y et al.
- **Journal:** Women's Health Reports, 2026
- **PMID:** 42564420
- **DOI:** [10.1177/26884844261476393](https://doi.org/10.1177/26884844261476393)
- **Domain:** Queries 2, 4 (Perimenopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Demonstrates that a brief gynecologist-delivered counseling intervention at the workplace improves menopausal symptoms and work productivity — relevant to ALIN's occupational health angle and the case for accessible digital support.

### Physical and Psychosocial Complications Faced by Postmenopausal Women with Type 1 Diabetes: A Cross-Sectional BETTER Analysis
- **Authors:** Bonhoure A et al.
- **Journal:** Diabetes Research and Clinical Practice, 2026
- **PMID:** 42551704
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.diabres.2026.113484](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.diabres.2026.113484)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (Cardiovascular / Comorbidities)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Quantifies the compounded burden of menopause in women with T1D — CVD, neuropathy, retinopathy, depression risks all significantly elevated postmenopause, highlighting a high-need ALIN user subgroup.

> Postmenopausal women with T1D had 3.3× higher CVD risk, 2.2× higher neuropathy risk, 2.6× higher fall risk, and 1.5× higher moderate-to-severe depressive symptom risk vs premenopausal counterparts. Antihypertensive and lipid-lowering prescriptions were paradoxically lower in the postmenopausal group.

### Association Between Candidate Genes and FRAX Scores in Postmenopausal Women
- **Authors:** Pepe J et al.
- **Journal:** Endocrine, 2026
- **PMID:** 42570140
- **DOI:** [10.1007/s12020-026-04739-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12020-026-04739-9)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (Postmenopausal / Bone health)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Multilocus genetic risk profiling (ESR1, OPG, PTH, VDR, COL1A1 SNPs) to personalize osteoporosis fracture risk — early-stage but directly relevant to ALIN's bone health and personalized risk content.

> In 54 Italian postmenopausal women, PTH + OPG haplotype combinations were associated with high FRAX scores. COL1A1, CASR, VDR haplotypes characterized lower-risk profiles. 20.3% had FRAX major fracture probability ≥20%; 51.8% had hip fracture probability ≥3%. Suggests multi-locus genetic analysis could refine personalized fracture prevention.

### Weight Loss and Omega-3 Supplementation Modulate the Microbiome in Women with Increased Breast Cancer Risk
- **Authors:** Cook KL et al.
- **Journal:** Cancer Prevention Research, 2026
- **PMID:** 41999613
- **DOI:** [10.1158/1940-6207.CAPR-26-0021](https://doi.org/10.1158/1940-6207.CAPR-26-0021)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (Breast cancer)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** RCT in peri/postmenopausal obese women showing omega-3 + behavioral weight loss shifts gut microbiome favorably and improves breast cancer biomarkers (estradiol, adiponectin:leptin, CRP) — relevant to ALIN's lifestyle and breast cancer risk education.

> 34 peri/postmenopausal women (BMI ≥28) randomized to omega-3 (3.25 g/day) vs placebo during weight loss intervention. Omega-3 group showed greatest reduction in Firmicutes:Bacteroidetes ratio, increased SCFA-producing microbes (Phocaeicola vulgatus, Alistipes putredinis), and correlated improvements in bioavailable estradiol and adiponectin:leptin ratio.
