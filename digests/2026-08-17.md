### Association Between Menopausal Hormone Therapy and Alzheimer Disease Neuropathology
- **Authors:** Bruno J et al.
- **Journal:** Neurology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42585606
- **DOI:** [10.1212/WNL.0000000000218413](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218413)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/menopause) · Query 4 (cognitive/dementia)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Provides direct neuropathological evidence for MHT's protective effect on Alzheimer's pathology — one of the strongest mechanistic links seen so far. ALIN can use this to address user anxiety about dementia risk and explain why timing and use of MHT matter.

> Women using estrogen-only MHT had significantly lower odds of increased Alzheimer's pathology at autopsy (OR 0.65, 95% CI 0.48–0.88) and lower amyloid burden on plasma and CSF biomarkers in two independent large cohorts (NACC, n=2,959; ADNI, n=2,058). MHT users also had significantly lower odds of clinical dementia diagnosis (OR 0.61) and memory/functional decline (OR 0.67).

### Causal Associations Between Hormone Replacement Therapy and Brain Structure
- **Authors:** Zhu X, Liang Y, Tao T, Huang L
- **Journal:** Progress in Neuropsychopharmacology & Biological Psychiatry, 2026
- **PMID:** 42575188
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.pnpbp.2026.111885](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pnpbp.2026.111885)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Adds causal (Mendelian randomization + machine learning) evidence that HRT timing affects specific brain regions. Supports messaging around the importance of when to start HRT, beyond just symptom relief.

> Using Mendelian randomization and double machine learning in the UK Biobank (n=~1,366 brain phenotypes), later initiation of HRT was associated with lower white-matter integrity, while HRT ever-use was causally linked to larger right inferior frontal gyrus and nucleus accumbens volumes. These associations were FDR-controlled and independently replicated.

### The Menopause Transition and Epilepsy: A Focus on Therapeutics
- **Authors:** Smith KM, Faubion SS, Vadlamudi L et al.
- **Journal:** Neurology: Clinical Practice, 2026
- **PMID:** 42594325
- **DOI:** [10.1212/CPJ.0000000000200650](https://doi.org/10.1212/CPJ.0000000000200650)
- **Domain:** Queries 1, 2, 4
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Opens up an underserved subgroup — women with epilepsy navigating perimenopause. HRT may affect seizure control and lamotrigine metabolism. ALIN should be aware of this interaction when supporting users who mention epilepsy medications.

> HRT may influence seizure control and the metabolism of antiseizure medications such as lamotrigine. Data on HRT use in women with epilepsy are limited, which may be deterring clinicians from prescribing it. The review calls for more research and highlights non-hormonal options as alternatives.

### Associations of Iron Indicators with Cognitive Impairment Risk in Postmenopausal Women
- **Authors:** Chao W, Liu P, Li J et al.
- **Journal:** Molecular Neurobiology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42593708
- **DOI:** [10.1007/s12035-026-06129-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12035-026-06129-8)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cognitive)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Identifies hepcidin and total iron binding capacity as modifiable biomarkers for cognitive impairment risk in postmenopausal women — a novel non-hormonal angle on cognitive health that ALIN could incorporate into lifestyle and nutrition guidance.

> In 1,344 postmenopausal women aged >60 years, elevated hepcidin was associated with increased cognitive impairment risk (OR 1.52). Non-linear association with TIBC (inflection at 100.69 µmol/L). BPNN model found hepcidin (95.5% importance) and TIBC (79.1%) as strongest predictors alongside albumin, HDL, and reproductive lifespan.

### Evidence for Actions of Estrone Distinct from Those of Estradiol
- **Authors:** Santen RJ, Gompel A, Klein KO, Lumsden MA, Pinkerton JV, Stuenkel CA, Yue W
- **Journal:** Journal of Clinical Endocrinology and Metabolism, 2026
- **PMID:** 42161325
- **DOI:** [10.1210/clinem/dgag213](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgag213)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cognitive/breast cancer)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Challenges the assumption that estrone (E1) is biologically inert. If E1 has distinct brain, breast, and immune effects, it has implications for choosing oral vs. transdermal HRT (oral routes generate high E1). ALIN may need to update messaging about "all estrogens are equivalent."

> Postmenopausal plasma E1 is 4-fold higher than E2 and produced at 8-fold higher rates. New evidence shows E1 has distinct effects on brain function, gene expression, breast tissue inflammation, immune modulation, and vascular endothelial cells — independent of conversion to E2. Clinical implications for type of estrogen therapy selection, especially for cognitive function and mood disorders, require further study.

### Estrogen Deficiency-Induced Lipid Dysregulation in Menopause: Mechanisms, Metabolic Consequences, and Therapeutic Strategies
- **Authors:** Sophian A
- **Journal:** Journal of Steroid Biochemistry and Molecular Biology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42580603
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.jsbmb.2026.107102](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsbmb.2026.107102)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Comprehensive mechanistic review of how estrogen loss drives atherogenic lipid changes — useful for ALIN content explaining *why* cardiovascular risk rises at menopause and what HRT does to lipid metabolism.

> Estrogen deficiency disrupts hepatic lipid metabolism, alters lipoprotein composition, promotes visceral adiposity, and triggers chronic low-grade inflammation, establishing an atherogenic lipid profile. HRT, lifestyle modifications, lipid-lowering pharmacotherapy, and novel molecular targets are discussed. LPL activity, hepatic lipid accumulation, and ER signaling in adipose/liver are key mechanisms.

### Systemic Menopausal Hormone Therapy Treatment Patterns in Danish Women — A Nationwide Drug Utilization Study
- **Authors:** Wood-Kurland HK, Sørensen KK, Møller AL et al.
- **Journal:** British Journal of Clinical Pharmacology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42575876
- **DOI:** [10.1002/bcp.70773](https://doi.org/10.1002/bcp.70773)
- **Domain:** Queries 1, 4
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Real-world data showing a 2024 rebound in MHT initiation after decades of decline, and a shift toward transdermal therapy (75% in 2024). Confirms the "menopause moment" trend that is driving ALIN's market opportunity.

> In 2.35 million Danish women, MHT prevalence declined from 107.7/1,000 in 2000 to 17.8/1,000 in 2024, but new initiation rebounded sharply in 2024 (8.5/1,000 person-years, up from 4.3 in 2023). By 2024, 75% of new initiators used transdermal therapy. Cumulative duration of use was often <1 year, suggesting ongoing undertreatment.

- **DOI:** [10.1007/s11886-026-02400-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11886-026-02400-5)
- **Domain:** Queries 4, 5
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Reviews the J-shaped relationship between endogenous androgens and CVD mortality, and the route-dependency of testosterone therapy risks. Supports ALIN messaging that the estrogen/androgen ratio matters as much as absolute levels.
