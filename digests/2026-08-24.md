### Hormone Therapy in POI and Early Menopause: A New FIGO-IMS Paradigm (Position Paper)
- **Authors:** Benedetto C et al.
- **Journal:** International Journal of Gynaecology & Obstetrics, 2026
- **PMID:** 42609071
- **DOI:** [10.1002/ijgo.71217](https://doi.org/10.1002/ijgo.71217)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT) & Query 4 (cardiovascular/cognitive)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Landmark joint FIGO and IMS position paper calling for HT in POI/early menopause to be reframed as hormonal replacement for a chronic endocrine condition — directly actionable for ALIN guidance on younger users with POI/early menopause.

> POI and early menopause should be recognized as chronic endocrine conditions and sentinel events for accelerated aging. HT should be reframed as hormonal replacement aimed at preventing long-term disability, with timely initiation, adequate dosing, and long-term continuation considered foundational. FIGO and IMS call for globally coordinated efforts and equitable access to multidisciplinary care.

### Estradiol/Dydrogesterone MHT Literature Review: Personalization Across BMI Groups
- **Authors:** Nappi RE et al.
- **Journal:** Climacteric, 2026
- **PMID:** 42610382
- **DOI:** [10.1080/13697137.2026.2709302](https://doi.org/10.1080/13697137.2026.2709302)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Reviews evidence for estradiol/dydrogesterone (Femoston) across BMI subgroups; dydrogesterone's favorable VTE and breast cancer risk profile relative to other progestogens is useful for ALIN's nuanced MHT guidance.

> Ultra-low and standard doses of E/D provide effective relief from estrogen-depletion symptoms, high amenorrhea rates, and fracture risk reduction with no new safety concerns. In women with high BMI, effective symptom relief was maintained with no significant BMI-related difference in adverse events. Dydrogesterone may confer advantages in VTE and breast cancer risk vs. other progestogens.

### UK Biobank: HRT and Elevated ASCVD Risk — Reproductive Factors Matter
- **Authors:** Wang M et al.
- **Journal:** Acta Obstetricia et Gynecologica Scandinavica, 2026
- **PMID:** 42619552
- **DOI:** [10.1111/aogs.70316](https://doi.org/10.1111/aogs.70316)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT) & Query 4 (cardiovascular)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Large UK Biobank prospective study (n=170,250) showing HRT associated with elevated ASCVD risk — especially elevated for perimenopause users and women ≤45 years. Supports ALIN's emphasis on individualized cardiovascular risk assessment before and during MHT.

> HRT users showed elevated ASCVD risk (adjusted HR 1.13, 95% CI 1.08–1.18), primarily driven by ischemic stroke. Perimenopause showed most pronounced elevation (HR 1.28). Women ≤45 years had 77% higher ASCVD risk with HRT use. Dose-response relationship with duration ≥5 years (p=0.009). Minimal mediation through standard biomarkers (<2%), suggesting direct vascular mechanisms.

### Menopause and the Liver-Kidney-Metabolic Axis: MHT Effects Are Context-Dependent
- **Authors:** Jamalinia M, Lonardo A, Weiskirchen R
- **Journal:** Advances in Therapy, 2026
- **PMID:** 42629533
- **DOI:** [10.1007/s12325-026-03758-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12325-026-03758-2)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT) & Query 4 (cardiovascular)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Novel review framing menopause as a systems-level modifier of liver-kidney-metabolic (LKM) health; MHT's variable effects on MASLD and CKD are formulation-, timing-, and metabolic-phenotype-dependent — adds nuance for ALIN's MHT safety messaging.

> Menopause disrupts the liver-kidney-metabolic axis through altered fat distribution, insulin sensitivity, inflammation, and hepatic/renal physiology. Advanced MASLD may accelerate CKD through insulin resistance, inflammation, gut dysbiosis, and nephrotoxic metabolites. MHT does not have current evidence to support use for MASLD or CKD prevention, but personalized strategies targeting CKM health may support holistic peri/postmenopausal care.

### Fezolinetant Real-World Safety: Liver and Neurological Signals in WHO VigiAccess
- **Authors:** Li Y et al.
- **Journal:** Menopause, 2026
- **PMID:** 42610452
- **DOI:** [10.1097/GME.0000000000002887](https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000002887)
- **Domain:** Query 3 (fezolinetant/non-hormonal)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** First pharmacovigilance analysis of fezolinetant using WHO global database (n=1,091 reports) — confirms real-world liver signal and neuropsychiatric signals (insomnia, brain fog). Critical for ALIN's guidance on the non-hormonal NK3 antagonist option.

> 42 safety signals were consistently detected across all disproportionality methods. Strongest signals: menopausal symptoms (likely disease) and facial discomfort. Liver-related signals clustered: elevated ALT (ROR=20.60), AST (ROR=22.95), bilirubin (ROR=12.52). Neurological/psychiatric events included insomnia, headache, dizziness, brain fog, and sleep disorder. Real-world safety broadly consistent with phase 3 trials.

### Age at Menopause and Cognitive Symptoms Predict Objective Cognitive Outcomes
- **Authors:** Facchetti T et al. (Columbia University)
- **Journal:** Journal of Clinical and Experimental Neuropsychology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42616473
- **DOI:** [10.1080/13803395.2026.2707992](https://doi.org/10.1080/13803395.2026.2707992)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cognitive/dementia)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Demonstrates that subjective cognitive symptoms plus age at menopause predict objective digital cognitive test performance — supports ALIN's integration of both symptom tracking and timing of menopause for Alzheimer's risk stratification.

> 48.1% of postmenopausal women reported ≥1 cognitive symptom. Subjective cognitive score was negatively associated with objective cognition (DCR™ digital test). Age at menopause showed significant quadratic association with cognitive performance — younger and older age at menopause both predicted lower scores. Routine cognitive screening at Well-Woman visits is supported.

### Menopausal Transition Does NOT Cause Clinically Meaningful Thyroid Dysfunction
- **Authors:** Hayashi S et al. (St. Luke's International Hospital, Tokyo)
- **Journal:** Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism, 2026
- **PMID:** 42615120
- **DOI:** [10.1210/clinem/dgag337](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgag337)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cardiovascular/metabolic)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Large longitudinal study (n=7,644 Japanese women, 86,967 visits) clarifies that thyroid abnormalities increasing at midlife are driven by age rather than menopause per se — resolves common confusion for ALIN users presenting with fatigue/cognitive symptoms.

> TSH increased 0.014 mIU/L per year before menopause with no significant slope change at menopause. fT4 declined before menopause then stabilized thereafter. Prevalence of abnormal TSH and thyroid disorders increased with age but showed no distinct change at menopause. Age-related factors, not menopausal transition, largely explain increasing thyroid abnormalities in midlife women.

### Vasomotor Symptoms and Depression Are Primary Sleep Drivers in Postmenopausal Women
- **Authors:** Kim I, Park NJ
- **Journal:** Cancer Nursing, 2026
- **PMID:** 42608713
- **DOI:** [10.1097/NCC.0000000000001630](https://doi.org/10.1097/NCC.0000000000001630)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cardiovascular/breast cancer) & Query 2 (perimenopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Confirms vasomotor symptoms and depressive symptoms (not cancer history) are the primary predictors of poor sleep in postmenopausal women — directly supports ALIN's integrated hot flash/mood/sleep tracking approach.

> Vasomotor symptoms (β=0.22, p=0.004) and depressive symptoms (β=0.45, p<0.001) together with employment status and smoking explained 54% of variance in global PSQI. BC survivorship was not independently associated with sleep quality. Musculoskeletal pain became non-significant after accounting for depression. Targeted interdisciplinary management of vasomotor and depressive symptoms is recommended.

### Vaginal CO₂ Laser for GSM in Breast Cancer Survivors: Dose-Determining Study
- **Authors:** Jacobsen S et al. (Randers Regional Hospital, Denmark)
- **Journal:** Lasers in Surgery and Medicine, 2026
- **PMID:** 42619221
- **DOI:** [10.1002/lsm.70187](https://doi.org/10.1002/lsm.70187)
- **Domain:** Query 3 (non-hormonal) & Query 5 (genitourinary)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Dose-response study for CO₂ vaginal laser in breast cancer survivors on endocrine therapy (n=30); optimal benefit seen after 5 sessions — supports ALIN's non-hormonal GSM treatment guidance for users in whom vaginal estrogen is contraindicated.

> Vaginal dryness decreased significantly after 3 treatments with further incremental reduction by the 5th session representing the largest session-dependent change. FSFI scores increased significantly with additional improvements across later sessions. No serious adverse events. Five-session protocol was feasible and acceptable for BCSs on endocrine therapy.

### Adiposity and Breast Cancer Risk in 280,000 Chinese Women: Premenopausal and Postmenopausal
- **Authors:** Kartsonaki C et al. (University of Oxford)
- **Journal:** International Journal of Epidemiology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42618884
- **DOI:** [10.1093/ije/dyag155](https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyag155)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (breast cancer)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Large China Kadoorie Biobank (n=284,538) showing that — unlike Western data — higher BMI increases breast cancer risk even in premenopausal Chinese women; adiposity associated with ER+ but not ER− breast cancer postmenopausally. Relevant for ALIN's diverse user base weight management messaging.

> Higher BMI was associated with higher breast cancer risk in postmenopausal (HR=1.35) AND premenopausal women (HR=1.13), contrasting with inverse association seen in Western premenopausal populations. Among postmenopausal women, adiposity associated with ER+ (HR=1.47) but not ER− breast cancer. Waist circumference and fat percentage also associated with higher risk.

- **Relevance to ALIN:** Clinical review covering phenotypic age and epigenetic/DNA methylation clocks in relation to menopause — no validated threshold for routine clinical use yet, but directly relevant as ALIN explores biological aging features.

### AMH Reference Values for Perimenopause: Linking Low AMH to Vasomotor Symptom Severity
- **Authors:** Xu K et al. (Shandong University)
- **Journal:** Chinese Medical Journal, 2026
- **PMID:** 42608648
- **DOI:** [10.1097/CM9.0000000000004161](https://doi.org/10.1097/CM9.0000000000004161)
- **Domain:** Query 2 (perimenopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Nationally representative Chinese cohort (n=10,543) establishing first standardized AMH reference percentiles for women aged 34-56 using ultrasensitive assay; lower AMH percentile associated with more severe vasomotor symptoms — potential biomarker for ALIN symptom prediction.

> Ultrasensitive AMH assay enabled menopause diagnosis (AUC=0.72) and detected AMH in most women aged 44-49 years. Women with baseline AMH below the 10th percentile experienced faster decline before age 40. Lower AMH percentiles were associated with more severe perimenopausal symptoms (β=0.12), particularly vasomotor symptoms.

### Diabetes and Menopause: Bidirectional Relationship and Unmet Clinical Need
- **Authors:** Black R et al. (Ulster University)
- **Journal:** Diabetic Medicine, 2026
- **PMID:** 42632082
- **DOI:** [10.1111/dme.70420](https://doi.org/10.1111/dme.70420)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT) & Query 2 (perimenopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Scoping review synthesizing evidence on diabetes-menopause interaction; HRT was linked to lower HbA1c levels but is largely overlooked — important for ALIN's comorbidity guidance for users with T1DM or T2DM navigating menopause.

> 22 studies included. Women with diabetes experience similar menopausal symptoms but may be at increased severity risk. HRT was linked to lower HbA1c but is largely overlooked in diabetes-menopause care. Women with diabetes are ill-informed and lack effective support. Hypothesized bidirectional relationship: progression of one may be influenced by the other.
