### Management of Menopausal Hormone Therapy
- **Authors:** Switzer J et al.
- **Journal:** Current Opinion in Obstetrics & Gynecology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42563637
- **DOI:** [10.1097/GCO.0000000000001135](https://doi.org/10.1097/GCO.0000000000001135)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT/menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Practical, up-to-date review on MHT risk/benefit framing that ALIN can draw on for evidence-based educational content; directly addresses timing hypothesis, transdermal estradiol + micronized progesterone safety, and mood/cognition.

> Reanalysis of WHI confirms protective cardiovascular role of estrogen initiated before age 60; transdermal estradiol + micronized progesterone does not increase VTE risk; MHT is safe and effective for vasomotor symptoms in healthy women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause. Major societies do not yet recommend MHT for primary prevention.

### Perimenopause and Metabolic Vulnerability: Hormones, Body Composition and Lifestyle Changes
- **Authors:** Lobato VA et al.
- **Journal:** Climacteric, 2026
- **PMID:** 42634879
- **DOI:** [10.1080/13697137.2026.2713394](https://doi.org/10.1080/13697137.2026.2713394)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (menopause/cardiovascular/metabolic)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Frames perimenopause as a "critical window of metabolic vulnerability" — a key narrative for ALIN; covers visceral fat accumulation, insulin resistance, and lifestyle interventions (resistance training, Mediterranean diet) even without weight change.

> Perimenopause is marked by increased visceral fat, loss of lean mass, reduced fat oxidation and impaired metabolic flexibility even without major weight gain. Declining estrogen drives adipose redistribution, hepatic insulin resistance and chronic inflammation. Resistance training and Mediterranean diet show strong potential to preserve muscle and reduce inflammation.

### Women's and Health Professionals' Needs and Preferences Informing the Updated IMS Recommendations
- **Authors:** Vincent AJ et al.

- **PMID:** 42635099
- **DOI:** [10.1080/13697137.2026.2715963](https://doi.org/10.1080/13697137.2026.2715963)

### The Grandmother Hypothesis: A Perspective from Cardiovascular Aging
- **Authors:** Zhang W
- **Journal:** Ageing Research Reviews, 2026
- **PMID:** 42637013
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.arr.2026.103334](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.arr.2026.103334)
- **Domain:** Queries 1, 4 (HRT/cardiovascular)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Evolutionary reframing of menopause as mismatch between ancestral selection pressures and modern longevity — compelling narrative for ALIN; explains postmenopausal cardiovascular risk increase and argues for HRT timing-based intervention.

### Incretin-Based Therapies in Peri- and Postmenopausal Women with Obesity: An Expert Position Statement from the Spanish Menopause Society
- **Authors:** Sánchez-Prieto M et al.
- **Journal:** Maturitas, 2026
- **PMID:** 42664617
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.maturitas.2026.109101](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.maturitas.2026.109101)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (postmenopausal/cardiovascular/metabolic)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** GLP-1/incretin therapies (semaglutide, tirzepatide) are increasingly used by perimenopausal women; this expert statement clarifies that concomitant MHT should not be initiated solely to augment weight loss, and sets a cardiometabolic + musculoskeletal monitoring framework.

### Vaginal High-Intensity Focused Ultrasound in Women with Genitourinary Syndrome of Menopause: A Prospective Exploratory Study

- **Journal:** Sexual Medicine, 2026
- **PMID:** 42667009
- **DOI:** [10.1093/sexmed/qfag077](https://doi.org/10.1093/sexmed/qfag077)
- **Domain:** Query 5 (vaginal estrogen/genitourinary syndrome/menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Emerging non-hormonal treatment for GSM relevant to women who cannot or choose not to use hormones; HIFU showed significant symptom reduction over 24 weeks, with stable microbiome and no serious adverse events.

- **PMID:** 42647142
- **DOI:** [10.1097/GCO.0000000000001127](https://doi.org/10.1097/GCO.0000000000001127)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Underrecognized condition affecting 2–5% of postmenopausal women; HRT after surgical or premature menopause is recommended but optimal regimens unclear — important nuance for ALIN's personalized HRT guidance features.

### Curcumin Decreases Blood Pressure in an Obese Postmenopausal Mouse Model via NMDA Receptor Modulation
- **Authors:** John K et al. (Barnes MJ senior author)
- **Journal:** Journal of Nutrition, 2026
- **PMID:** 42665097
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.tjnut.2026.101797](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tjnut.2026.101797)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (postmenopausal/cardiovascular)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Mechanistic mouse study suggesting curcumin may target a postmenopausal-specific pathway (NMDA receptor phosphorylation in cardioregulatory brain regions) to lower blood pressure; relevant for ALIN's coverage of evidence-based lifestyle/supplement approaches.

> In obese ovariectomized mice on high-fat diet, 4 weeks of curcumin (300 mg/day) significantly reduced systolic BP, body weight, and blood glucose; curcumin attenuated NMDA receptor phosphorylation in the paraventricular nucleus and RVLM. ### Sex Differences in the Gut Microbiome and Related Metabolites: Role in Cardiometabolic Disease
- **Authors:** Wang Y et al.
- **Journal:** Gut Microbes, 2026
- **PMID:** 42647164
- **DOI:** [10.1080/19490976.2026.2721752](https://doi.org/10.1080/19490976.2026.2721752)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (postmenopausal/cardiovascular)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Synthesizes evidence that the postmenopausal shift in gut microbiota mediates increased cardiometabolic risk via SCFAs, bile acids, and TMAO; supports ALIN's gut-health content and reinforces that menopause is a systemic metabolic event, not just hormonal.

> Women generally have more favorable cardiometabolic risk than men during reproductive years, but risk significantly increases after menopause. Sexually dimorphic gut microbiota and metabolites (SCFAs, secondary bile acids, TMAO) contribute to these sex disparities. Sex steroids are the central driver. Large longitudinal studies with repeated microbiome + metabolomics measurements are needed.

### Age Differences in Clitoral-Vestibular Bulb Anatomy Across the Adult Lifespan
- **Authors:** Bowen ST et al.
- **Journal:** American Journal of Obstetrics and Gynecology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42660214
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.ajog.2026.08.030](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajog.2026.08.030)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (postmenopausal/cognitive — sexual function)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** First normative data on clitoral-vestibular bulb anatomy across the lifespan; documents age-related atrophy and caudal descent primarily affecting vestibular bulbs, providing anatomical basis for sexual function changes at menopause that ALIN can reference.

> In 134 women (18–80 years), increasing age was associated with shorter clitoral body length, smaller vestibular bulb volume, and more caudal positioning. Aging primarily affected vestibular bulbs (not clitoral volume). Findings suggest age-related atrophy and descent that may explain sexual function changes and other pelvic symptoms at midlife.

- **Relevance to ALIN:** GLP-1 agonists are increasingly used by perimenopausal women; this case illustrates their potential for hormone-therapy-related metabolic complications — relevant to ALIN's growing content on breast cancer survivorship and metabolic health.

- **Authors:** Salama Y, Heikal N

- **PMID:** 42639562
- **DOI:** [10.7759/cureus.115096](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.115096)

- **Relevance to ALIN:** Documents bone density recovery through lifestyle alone (osteogenic loading, resistance training, diet, supplementation) in a cancer survivor who declined alendronate — supports ALIN's content on non-pharmacological bone health strategies.

### Dibutyl Phthalate Disrupts ESR1-GPX4 Redox Signaling and Aggravates Ovarian Dysfunction in PCOS-Like Mouse Model
- **Authors:** Zhang Y et al.
- **Journal:** Ecotoxicology and Environmental Safety, 2026
- **PMID:** 42660033
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.ecoenv.2026.120714](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecoenv.2026.120714)
- **Domain:** Query 5 (DHEA/ovarian function)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Phthalate exposure and ovarian function disruption connects to broader ALIN themes of environmental health and perimenopause onset; exploratory clinical cohort shows higher phthalate levels in PCOS women — relevant for ALIN's environmental toxin content.
