### The Cardiometabolic Effects of Menopause
- **Authors:** Karam J et al.
- **Journal:** Obstetrics and Gynecology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42691325
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1097/AOG.0000000000006418
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cardiovascular/menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Clinical Expert Series from Mayo Clinic framing menopause as a critical window for cardiometabolic risk — directly actionable for ALIN's cardiovascular risk messaging to users. Covers VMS, sleep, lipids, blood pressure, and body composition trajectories.

> Menopause represents a critical window for adverse cardiometabolic and vascular changes extending beyond chronological aging, including increases in LDL-C, triglycerides, blood pressure, glucose, and body fat. Authors recommend incorporating menopause-specific factors into routine cardiovascular risk assessment.

### Menopausal Hormone Therapy and Cardiovascular Risk: Current Evidence and Treatment Recommendations
- **Authors:** Pillai AA et al.
- **Journal:** Cardiology in Review, 2026
- **PMID:** 42693520
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1097/CRD.0000000000001464
- **Domain:** Queries 1, 4 (HRT/MHT; cardiovascular)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Synthesizes current evidence post-2025 FDA boxed warning removal, providing tiered prescribing guidance by age and formulation — useful for ALIN's content on MHT safety and timing.

> Following the 2025 FDA label changes, this review confirms transdermal estradiol + micronized progesterone as the preferred formulation for women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause with low ASCVD risk and coronary artery calcium score of zero. Combined MHT is atherosclerosis-neutral in ages 50–59 but carries hazard ratio of 3.22 for atherosclerotic events in women aged 70–79.

### Menopausal 'Brain Fog' or Dementia? — A Practical Guide for Clinicians
- **Authors:** Abernethy K, Gurvich C, Spector A
- **Journal:** Post Reproductive Health (BMS Tool for Clinicians), 2026
- **PMID:** 42678390
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1080/20533691.2026.2705046
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cognitive/dementia)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** British Menopause Society clinical tool directly addressing one of ALIN users' most distressing concerns — whether their cognitive symptoms are menopause-related or early dementia. Provides clear criteria for history-taking, red flags, and when to refer.

> Brain fog around menopause is typically subjective, fluctuating, and associated with sleep disturbance and VMS, while dementia is progressive, objectively impairing, and causes functional decline. Hormone therapy is highlighted as a consideration when modifiable factors don't resolve symptoms.

### Microvascular Endothelial Function Is Blunted in Postmenopausal Females with Frequent Hot Flushes
- **Authors:** Stokes W et al.
- **Journal:** Experimental Physiology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42681820
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1113/EP093733
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cardiovascular/menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** First study to show that frequent hot flushes are associated with reduced *microvascular* (not just macrovascular) endothelial function, strengthening the mechanistic link between VMS severity and cardiovascular disease risk — important for ALIN's messaging that hot flashes are more than a nuisance.

> Postmenopausal women with ≥3 hot flushes/day had significantly lower reactive hyperaemia index (RHI 1.87 vs 2.31) compared to matched women without hot flushes. Reduced microvascular endothelial function likely contributes to greater cardiovascular disease development in this group.

### Bilateral Oophorectomy and Heart Failure Risk: Mechanisms, Evidence, and Clinical Implications
- **Authors:** Jafri DZ et al.
- **Journal:** Cardiology in Review, 2026
- **PMID:** 42679310
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1097/CRD.0000000000001457
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cardiovascular/menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Comprehensive review connecting surgical menopause to HFpEF risk — directly relevant to ALIN users who underwent oophorectomy for cancer prevention (e.g., BRCA carriers). Greatest risk seen in premenopausal bilateral oophorectomy, and ERT may mitigate some risk.

> Abrupt estrogen loss post-oophorectomy contributes to endothelial dysfunction, increased vascular stiffness, adverse metabolic/inflammatory changes, and myocardial remodeling — mechanisms overlapping substantially with HFpEF pathophysiology. The authors propose bilateral oophorectomy as an underrecognized female-specific cardiovascular risk factor.

### Biotics in Menopausal Health: A Two-Decade Bibliometric Analysis and Narrative Review
- **Authors:** Olawale F et al.
- **Journal:** Current Nutrition Reports, 2026
- **PMID:** 42700191
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s13668-026-00798-6
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cardiovascular/menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Reviews the growing evidence for gut microbiome modulation (probiotics, prebiotics, synbiotics) in menopause — maps the gut-menopause axis and evidence for bone, metabolic, cardiovascular, and VMS benefit. Novel framework for ALIN lifestyle recommendations.

### Menopausal Timing and Senescent-Immune Coupling in Age-Related Lobular Involution of the Human Breast
- **Authors:** Lue JC et al.
- **Journal:** EBioMedicine, 2026
- **PMID:** 42673763
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ebiom.2026.106463
- **Domain:** Query 4 (breast cancer/menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Longitudinal cohort showing that the menopausal transition conditions whether immune programmes clear senescent breast cells (reducing cancer risk) or permit persistent risk-associated tissue. Perimenopause identified as a critical immunological window for breast tissue remodeling.

> In 81 women with paired biopsies, SASP/innate immune programmes predicted *slower* involution (higher risk tissue) during perimenopause but *faster* clearance postmenopause. The perimenopausal "stall" architecture involves spatially uncoupled immune surveillance. Biomarker interpretation for breast cancer risk must be anchored to menopausal timing.

### Development and Bioavailability Testing of an Estetrol and Estetrol-Zinc-Containing Vaginal Hydrogel
- **Authors:** Takacs P et al.
- **Journal:** Menopause, 2026
- **PMID:** 41805135
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000002759
- **Domain:** Query 5 (vaginal estrogen/GSM)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Novel estetrol (E4) vaginal formulation with hydroxyethyl cellulose and HPβCD showing localized controlled release and minimal systemic exposure — extends ALIN's coverage of emerging GSM treatments beyond existing vaginal estradiol/prasterone options.

> The estetrol-HEC hydrogel with HPβCD demonstrated excellent biocompatibility (no cytotoxicity), confirmed estetrol retention within vaginal tissues (reducing systemic exposure), and sustained controlled drug release. Phase-solubility showed stable 1:1 E4:HPβCD complexation. Clinical trials required to confirm in vivo efficacy.

### Menopause-Specific Associations of Organophosphate Esters with Sex Hormones Among US Women
- **Authors:** Zheng Y, Fang L
- **Journal:** Steroids, 2026
- **PMID:** 42700785
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.steroids.2026.109851
- **Domain:** Query 5 (testosterone/postmenopausal women)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** NHANES-based study showing that common flame retardant/plasticizer chemicals (OPEs) lower testosterone and free androgen index in postmenopausal women — relevant to ALIN's environmental health content and to discussions of low libido/sexual dysfunction beyond hormonal aging.

> In 657 postmenopausal women, BCEP and BDCPP were negatively associated with testosterone and free androgen index. OPE mixtures as a whole also negatively associated with androgen levels. In premenopausal women, effects were different (positive SHBG association). Adds environmental endocrine disruption as a modifiable factor in postmenopausal androgen decline.

### Menopausal Experience in Solid-Organ Transplant Recipients
- **Authors:** Galasso F et al.
- **Journal:** Progress in Transplantation, 2026
- **PMID:** 42684863
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/15269248261481138
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Characterizes menopausal symptom burden and very low MHT use (8.9%) among 192 transplant recipients — highlights a specific underserved population. Useful context for ALIN's scope as an inclusive companion addressing medically complex users.

> Menopausal symptoms among SOT recipients are common (mild-to-moderate on MRS) but MHT utilization is strikingly low. Lack of increased vasomotor burden vs. literature may reflect under-recognition or misattribution to transplant-related factors. Recommends menopause-specific screening integrated into transplant care.

### Sex Hormones and Menopause Hormone Therapy Predict Muscle Strength Across the Lifespan
- **Authors:** Logue Cook RN et al.
- **Journal:** Journal of Applied Physiology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42518302
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.00366.2026
- **Domain:** Query 5 (testosterone/menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** NHANES-based study showing MHT users have ~1.9 kg greater handgrip strength in older age, widening to 14.3% advantage by age ≥80 — adds musculoskeletal health to ALIN's MHT benefits content beyond VMS relief.

> Estradiol and bioavailable estrogen were the strongest hormone predictors of muscle strength in females during midlife and older age. Women with a history of MHT use had significantly greater handgrip strength than non-users, an advantage that widened with age (2.4% at 60 yr vs 14.3% at ≥80 yr).

### Targeting Estrogen to the Brain via DHED Does Not Seem to Protect Against Metabolic Dysfunction in Obese OVX Mice
- **Authors:** Camon C et al.
- **Journal:** Endocrinology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42678760
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1210/endocr/bqag082
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT - basic science)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Tests DHED, a brain-selective estrogen prodrug intended to provide menopausal benefits (VMS, cognition) without peripheral cancer risk — negative metabolic result at standard doses is informative for ALIN's future treatments tracking.

> DHED (a brain-selective E2 prodrug) at standard doses failed to improve metabolic health parameters or recapitulate hypothalamic responses of systemic E2 in obese OVX mice. Higher doses showed some brain estrogenic effects but still failed to improve metabolic health. Authors call for further investigation of CNS-specific estrogen delivery.
