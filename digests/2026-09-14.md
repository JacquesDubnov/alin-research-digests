### Menopausal Hormone Therapy and Cardiovascular Risk in Midlife Women With Vasomotor Symptoms
- **Authors:** Wang Z et al.
- **Journal:** JAMA Internal Medicine, 2026
- **PMID:** 42709460
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2026.2922
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT) & Query 4 (cardiovascular)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Directly addresses the question ALIN users ask most — is MHT safe for the heart? This study focuses on the perimenopausal window, where hormonal fluctuations and vasomotor symptoms are greatest, filling a gap left by older trials.

> Vasomotor symptoms in perimenopause are associated with increased future cardiovascular risk. Most prior trials focused on postmenopausal women; this study evaluates MHT and CVD risk specifically among perimenopausal women with vasomotor symptoms — a critical and underexplored population.

### Role of Coronary Artery Calcium in the Association Between Hormone Therapy Status and Incident Death and Cardiovascular Disease: The Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis
- **Authors:** Bishay R et al. (Budoff MJ senior author)
- **Journal:** Menopause, 2026
- **PMID:** 42709629
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000002891
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cardiovascular)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Provides real-world data on HT and mortality over 14 years. The finding that current HT users had 30% lower all-cause death risk — and that coronary artery calcium did not modify this — strengthens ALIN's evidence base for benefit-risk discussions.

> Among 3,235 postmenopausal women followed for 14.4 years, current HT use vs. never use was associated with significantly lower all-cause mortality (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.58–0.86). Addition of coronary artery calcium score to models did not meaningfully change this association.

### Estrogen Deprivation Exacerbates Alzheimer's Disease Pathology Through Neuronal CTSS Signaling
- **Authors:** Yang R et al.
- **Journal:** Journal of Neuroinflammation, 2026
- **PMID:** 42728627
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s12974-026-03970-5
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cognitive/dementia)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Identifies a molecular mechanism (CTSS signaling) linking estrogen loss at menopause to Alzheimer's pathology — potentially actionable for ALIN's dementia prevention messaging and discussions of cognitive protection.

> Ovariectomy in female 5xFAD mice significantly exacerbates amyloid beta and tau pathology. Estrogen deprivation activates neuronal cathepsin S (CTSS) signaling, establishing a mechanistic link between postmenopausal estrogen decline and the disproportionately higher AD risk observed in women.

### Pre-Diagnostic Circulating Carotenoids and Lethal Breast Cancer Risk
- **Authors:** Peng C et al. (Willett WC, Eliassen AH)
- **Journal:** American Journal of Clinical Nutrition, 2026
- **PMID:** 42710783
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajcnut.2026.101514
- **Domain:** Query 4 (breast cancer)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Dietary carotenoids (β-carotene, β-cryptoxanthin) are associated with 41–48% lower lethal breast cancer risk, with stronger effects in postmenopausal women. Supports ALIN's nutrition and lifestyle recommendations.

> Among 2,583 breast cancer cases and 3,516 controls from the Nurses' Health Study, highest vs. lowest quintile of β-carotene was associated with 41% lower lethal breast cancer risk (HR 0.59). Total carotenoids showed 48% lower risk, with stronger inverse associations for postmenopausal women.
