### Hormonal Options for Perimenopause: A Clinician Toolkit
- **Authors:** Piasta J & Zewail A
- **Journal:** Obstetrics and Gynecology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42743436
- **DOI:** [10.1097/AOG.0000000000006436](https://doi.org/10.1097/AOG.0000000000006436)
- **Domain:** Query 2 — Perimenopause symptoms/treatment
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Directly addresses the heterogeneous and poorly-standardized perimenopause management space that ALIN's users navigate; provides a practical framework for hormonal contraceptives vs. alternative strategies during perimenopause.

> "Perimenopause is a clinically heterogeneous stage of reproductive aging with unique physiology distinct from menopause. It is variably defined, lacks specific guidelines or practice recommendations, and can be challenging for clinicians to manage." The piece offers shared decision-making frameworks covering contraceptive needs alongside evolving cardiometabolic risk.

### Efficacy and Safety of MHT for Depressive Symptoms in Perimenopausal Women: Meta-Analysis
- **Authors:** Li Y, Sun Y, Bi Y, Yang L, Xu Y, Wu H, Ma X
- **Journal:** Journal of Affective Disorders, 2026
- **PMID:** 42061517
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.jad.2026.121892](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2026.121892)
- **Domain:** Query 2 — Perimenopause symptoms/treatment
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Mood and depression are top ALIN user concerns; this 12-RCT meta-analysis quantifies HRT's modest benefit on depressive symptoms and flags evidence gaps for shared-decision discussions.

> HRT associated with small reduction in depressive symptom severity vs. placebo (SMD = −0.23, 95% CI −0.43 to −0.03). Effect is modest and certainty of evidence limited; tibolone/STEAR subgroup showed larger (non-significant) estimates. Larger RCTs needed.

### Effect of Fezolinetant on Sleep, Health-Related Quality of Life and Work Productivity
- **Authors:** Cano A, Neal Perry G, Martins K, et al.
- **Journal:** Climacteric, 2026
- **PMID:** 42734219
- **DOI:** [10.1080/13697137.2026.2715238](https://doi.org/10.1080/13697137.2026.2715238)
- **Domain:** Query 3 — Non-hormonal/NK3 antagonists
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Sleep disruption is a central ALIN symptom domain; this post-hoc SKYLIGHT 1&2 analysis demonstrates fezolinetant's sleep benefits beyond VMS reduction, including work productivity gains—directly relevant to midlife women juggling careers.

> Fezolinetant 30 mg and 45 mg improved sleep disturbance vs. placebo at 12 weeks regardless of baseline sleep status. Improvements in VMS frequency/severity and sleep disturbance correlated with better work productivity scores, benefits maintained over 1 year.

### Estradiol Transdermal Spray for Menopausal Symptom Management: From RCT to Real-World Evidence
- **Authors:** Hirschberg AL, Stute P, Águas F, Fait T, Polo-Kantola P, et al.
- **Journal:** Maturitas, 2026
- **PMID:** 42753514
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.maturitas.2026.109121](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.maturitas.2026.109121)
- **Domain:** Query 1 — HRT/menopausal hormone therapy
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Supports ALIN's MHT education module; synthesises real-world evidence for the transdermal spray format (a user-friendly delivery option) across 4 countries, showing consistent 70–90% reduction in severe hot flushes with high satisfaction.

> Across Portugal, Germany, Nordic countries, and Czech Republic real-world studies: severe/very severe hot flushes decreased 70–90% with 1–2 sprays daily. Satisfaction and ease-of-use rated 80–90%. Transdermal route bypasses first-pass metabolism; neutral VTE effect confirmed. Broadly aligned with guideline recommendations.

### Rethinking MHT After Risk-Reducing Salpingo-Oophorectomy in Women at High Breast Cancer Risk
- **Authors:** Wong SM & Kotsopoulos J
- **Journal:** Obstetrics and Gynecology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42743437
- **DOI:** [10.1097/AOG.0000000000006429](https://doi.org/10.1097/AOG.0000000000006429)
- **Domain:** Query 1 — HRT/menopausal hormone therapy
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Many ALIN users with BRCA mutations face the RRSO decision; this piece challenges widespread MHT underuse in that population and introduces bazedoxifene/conjugated estrogen as a potential breast-cancer-neutral option worth evaluating.

> Women with BRCA1/2 mutations recommended RRSO 5–15 years before natural menopause face acute surgical menopause. MHT remains most effective for symptom management but is underused due to misinformation. Authors propose studying CE+bazedoxifene as a formulation that may reduce breast cancer risk while treating symptoms—potentially transformative for this population.

### Accessing Support for Menopause-Associated Cognitive Difficulties in UK Primary Care: Qualitative Analysis
- **Authors:** Fordham E, Proctor DL, He L, Spector A
- **Journal:** Post Reproductive Health, 2026
- **PMID:** 42741851
- **DOI:** [10.1080/20533691.2026.2719474](https://doi.org/10.1080/20533691.2026.2719474)
- **Domain:** Query 4 — Menopause/cognitive/dementia
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Validates a core ALIN use case—women experiencing cognitive symptoms feel dismissed and must self-advocate; this qualitative study maps the exact barriers ALIN helps address (under-informed HCPs, long waits, inconsistent information).

> Women (N=153, ages 42–60) reported healthcare professionals as under-informed and dismissive regarding cognitive difficulties. Systemic challenges: long wait times, limited treatment options, lack of follow-up, inconsistent information. Many resorted to self-advocacy or private healthcare. Finding: enhanced HCP training and shared decision-making are urgently needed.

### Heterogeneity in Lipoprotein(a) Profile Changes Across the Menopausal Transition
- **Authors:** Palmer CA, Avery CL, Ballantyne CM, et al.
- **Journal:** European Journal of Preventive Cardiology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42751767
- **DOI:** [10.1093/eurjpc/zwag496](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwag496)
- **Domain:** Query 4 — Menopause/cardiovascular
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Cardiovascular risk assessment is key for ALIN users considering MHT; this UK Biobank study reveals that a single lifetime Lp(a) measurement misses menopausal reclassification, especially for women with intermediate levels—directly actionable preventive advice.

> Women with intermediate pre-menopausal Lp(a) (75–125 nmol/L) who underwent menopause experienced median Lp(a) increase of 34.9 nmol/L—~4× greater than women who remained pre/postmenopausal. 56% crossed the ≥125 nmol/L high-risk threshold post-transition. Single lifetime measurement insufficient; re-testing at menopause recommended.

### Glucose Excursions Throughout the Reproductive Life Stages in Women with Diabetes
- **Authors:** Knorr S, Knudsen TS, Kirkegaard K, et al.
- **Journal:** Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism, 2026
- **PMID:** 42740693
- **DOI:** [10.1210/clinem/dgag379](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgag379)
- **Domain:** Query 1 — HRT/menopausal hormone therapy
- **Relevance to ALIN:** A growing ALIN user segment has type 1 or type 2 diabetes; this review addresses how MHT may improve glycaemia during menopause and identifies critical evidence gaps for this population.

> At menopause, declining estrogen and relatively preserved androgen levels increase insulin resistance. MHT may improve glycaemia, though evidence in T1DM is limited. CGM + detailed hormonal profiling needed. Calls for future studies integrating diabetes technology with MHT use across the female lifespan.

### The Role of Hormones in Female Androgenetic Alopecia: Focus on Menopause
- **Authors:** Anderson A, Konopelski P, Skorzewska K, et al.
- **Journal:** Climacteric, 2026
- **PMID:** 42755697
- **DOI:** [10.1080/13697137.2026.2699762](https://doi.org/10.1080/13697137.2026.2699762)
- **Domain:** Query 5 — Testosterone/DHEA/vaginal estrogen/menopause
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Hair loss is a common but under-addressed perimenopausal complaint in ALIN feedback; this review explains the hormonal pathogenesis (relative hyperandrogenism from declining estrogen) and identifies risk factors ALIN can surface for users.

> Female androgenetic alopecia increases with age due to menopausal hormonal changes—estrogen decline creates relative hyperandrogenism. Correlation between total/free testosterone and FAGA not consistently proven; adipose-tissue hormones and metabolic syndrome (insulin resistance, high BMI) are key modifiable risk factors.

### Treatment-Related Menopause in Cancer Survivors: Mechanisms and Management
- **Authors:** Yongue G, Nash Z, Manley K, Davies MC, Nicum S
- **Journal:** Reproduction & Fertility, 2026
- **PMID:** 42747249
- **DOI:** [10.1530/RAF-26-0082](https://doi.org/10.1530/RAF-26-0082)
- **Domain:** Query 4 — Menopause/cardiovascular/cancer
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Cancer survivors experiencing treatment-related menopause are an important ALIN user segment; this review confirms their symptoms are often severe, overlap with treatment side effects, and remain under-addressed.

> Cancer-related menopause causes sudden severe symptoms including hot flushes, poor sleep, mood disturbance, vaginal dryness, fatigue, and cognitive problems. Bone loss and cardiovascular disease are long-term concerns. Despite growing awareness, many survivors don't receive adequate information, support, or treatment. Personalised menopause management can improve long-term health and QoL.

### Inflammatory Biomarkers and Risk of Postmenopausal ER-Positive Breast Cancer
- **Authors:** Albers FE, Swain CT, Dashti SG, et al.
- **Journal:** British Journal of Cancer, 2026
- **PMID:** 42763311
- **DOI:** [10.1038/s41416-026-03624-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41416-026-03624-6)
- **Domain:** Query 4 — Menopause/breast cancer
- **Relevance to ALIN:** ALIN users frequently ask about breast cancer risk factors beyond HRT; this case-cohort study identifies leptin and IL-10 as novel risk markers for postmenopausal ER+ cancer, with implications for lifestyle guidance.

> Per doubling in circulating leptin: RR 1.13 (95% CI 1.03–1.25) for ER-positive breast cancer. IL-10 also significantly associated (RR 1.14). Adiponectin, TNF-α trended higher but not statistically significant. Systemic inflammation—often driven by obesity and metabolic syndrome—may play an important role in postmenopausal breast carcinogenesis.

### Harmonized International Reference Ranges for Plasma Estradiol in Postmenopausal Women
- **Authors:** Cui J, Hankinson SE, Matsumoto AM, Santen RJ, et al.
- **Journal:** Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism, 2026
- **PMID:** 42120349
- **DOI:** [10.1210/clinem/dgag188](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgag188)
- **Domain:** Query 4/5 — Postmenopausal/vaginal estrogen monitoring
- **Relevance to ALIN:** ALIN users monitoring estradiol levels (e.g., during vaginal therapy or after dose adjustments) need context for their numbers; this landmark multi-cohort study (n=7,206) establishes the first harmonised international reference ranges.

> Using CDC RMP-calibrated LC-MS/MS across 5 international cohorts: normal postmenopausal estradiol range 1.1–18.2 pg/mL (non-obese: 1.1–12.5 pg/mL). Levels correlate with BMI but not chronological age. Provides the clinical benchmark needed for monitoring systemic and local estradiol therapy and disease risk prediction.
