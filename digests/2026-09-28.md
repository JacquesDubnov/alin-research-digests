### Blood Proteomics of Menopause Map to Brain Aging and Dementia Risk
- **Authors:** Wood Alexander M et al.
- **Journal:** Nature Medicine, 2026
- **PMID:** 42773212
- **DOI:** [10.1038/s41591-026-04648-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04648-4)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cardiovascular/cognitive/dementia)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Landmark proteomics study directly linking menopause-driven biological changes to brain aging trajectories and dementia risk — supports ALIN's framing of menopause as a neurological transition state and informs conversations about cognitive health.

> Using blood proteomics across multiple cohorts (n=80 rigorously staged + validation in 2,814 women), spontaneous menopause was characterized by dysregulation in inflammatory, synaptic, metabolic, and Alzheimer's disease biological processes. These shifts tracked more with hormones than with age. In four independent cohorts of older women (total n=11,925), higher menopause proteomic scores associated consistently with cognitive aging and dementia risk.

### Contemporary Menopausal Hormone Therapy and Thrombotic Disease: Nationwide Nested Case-Control Study
- **Authors:** Berggreen J et al.
- **Journal:** BMJ, 2026
- **PMID:** 42778212
- **DOI:** [10.1136/bmj-2026-100688](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2026-100688)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT) + Query 4 (VTE/cardiovascular)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Large Danish nationwide study (9,807 VTE cases, 18,460 stroke cases, 11,974 MI cases) providing contemporary data on MHT thrombotic risk by route — confirms transdermal safety signal and updates oral estrogen risk quantification.

> Oral MHT (any dose/duration) was associated with increased VTE risk (HR 1.6) and prolonged use of oral estradiol >1 mg/day with elevated stroke (HR 1.8 at >5 years) and MI risk. Transdermal therapy showed no increased thrombotic risk overall. Absolute risk increase for VTE with oral MHT: 0.09% per year (NNH=1055/year).

- **Authors:** Benedetto C, Gulati M et al.
- **Journal:** Global Heart, 2026
- **PMID:** 42799440
- **DOI:** [10.5334/gh.1576](https://doi.org/10.5334/gh.1576)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT) + Query 4 (cardiovascular)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** International consensus guidance (FIGO + World Heart Federation) on MHT prescribing for women with elevated CVD risk — directly informs ALIN's ability to support users with complex cardiac history.

> Women with CVD risk factors should receive thorough risk stratification; transdermal estradiol with micronized progesterone or dydrogesterone is the safest regimen for high thromboembolic risk. Women with established ischemic heart disease may receive low-dose transdermal MHT for symptom relief with appropriate risk-benefit review.

### Dietary Interventions for Menopausal Symptoms: A Systematic Review

- **Journal:** The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition, 2026
- **PMID:** 42790873
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.ajcnut.2026.101539](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajcnut.2026.101539)
- **Domain:** Query 2 (perimenopause/treatment) + Query 4 (cognitive)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Systematic review of 15 RCTs evaluating dietary approaches (soy, DASH, flaxseed, intermittent fasting) for vasomotor and cognitive symptoms — informs ALIN's lifestyle/nutrition content.

> Soy-based interventions may modestly reduce hot flash frequency and severity (SMD = -0.79), but certainty of evidence is very low. Other dietary patterns (DASH, lacto-ovo-vegetarian, MIND) showed modest benefits for mood and cognition in narrative synthesis. Evidence remains insufficient for definitive dietary recommendations.

### Association of Menopausal Status and MHT With Cardiovascular Risk Factors in a Large French Urban Population
- **Authors:** Mansencal D et al.
- **Journal:** European Journal of Preventive Cardiology, 2026
- **PMID:** 40727976
- **DOI:** [10.1093/eurjpc/zwaf460](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwaf460)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT) + Query 4 (cardiovascular)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Large prospective cohort (n=7,395 women, 2010–2023) demonstrating that MHT use may attenuate the elevated cardiovascular risk burden associated with menopause.

> Menopausal women showed significantly higher age-adjusted odds of obesity, hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidaemia vs. non-menopausal women. However, menopausal women using MHT did not show significant differences in cardiovascular risk factor prevalence compared with non-menopausal women, suggesting a protective metabolic effect.

### Reproductive Characteristics, Postmenopausal Obesity, and Cardiovascular Disease Incidence in the Women's Health Initiative
- **Authors:** Borrowman JD et al.
- **Journal:** Journal of the American Heart Association, 2026
- **PMID:** 42786622
- **DOI:** [10.1161/JAHA.126.049882](https://doi.org/10.1161/JAHA.126.049882)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cardiovascular)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Large WHI analysis (n=119,182) confirming that early menarche, early menopause, and short reproductive lifespan independently predict CVD — waist circumference partially mediates these relationships.

> Early menarche (HR 1.07), early menopause (HR 1.22), and short reproductive lifespan (HR 1.21) were independently associated with CVD over median 12.8 years of follow-up. Waist circumference was a significant mediator, suggesting obesity management as a modifiable intervention target.

- **Domain:** Query 4 (cardiovascular/cancer — surgical menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Large international cohort (4,286 BRCA1 + 1,427 BRCA2 carriers) quantifying residual ovarian cancer risk by age at BSO — informs ALIN content on surgical menopause and risk-reducing decisions.

### Endocrine Therapy Adherence and 10-Year Cardiometabolic Risk in Hormone Receptor-Positive Breast Cancer
- **Authors:** Ledesma JR et al.
- **Journal:** JNCI Cancer Spectrum, 2026
- **PMID:** 42786642
- **DOI:** [10.1093/jncics/pkag095](https://doi.org/10.1093/jncics/pkag095)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (breast cancer)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Large Kaiser Permanente cohort (n=8,365) quantifying the cardiometabolic cost of adjuvant endocrine therapy in postmenopausal breast cancer survivors — relevant for ALIN users navigating cancer treatment and menopause.

> 5-year AI adherence was associated with increased 10-year incidence of hypertension (+8.81%), dyslipidaemia (+5.16%), and diabetes (+2.58%), but lower all-cause and breast cancer mortality. AI carried higher dyslipidaemia risk than tamoxifen (+9.86% difference). Findings support monitoring and prevention of cardiometabolic risk in women on AET.

- **DOI:** [10.1007/s00213-026-07171-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-026-07171-z)
- **Domain:** Query 2 (perimenopause/symptoms)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Preclinical study identifying a novel pharmacological target (α5-GABAA) that reverses dopamine dysregulation in perimenopause — potential future treatment pathway for psychiatric symptoms during perimenopause without standard antipsychotic side effects.

### Effects of Dietary Nitrate on Cardiovascular Reactivity in Normotensive Postmenopausal Women
- **Authors:** Lundebrekke M et al.
- **Journal:** European Journal of Applied Physiology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42782334
- **DOI:** [10.1007/s00421-026-06430-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-026-06430-3)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cardiovascular)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Small RCT (n=14) testing beetroot juice (800 mg nitrate/day × 4 days) on blood pressure reactivity in postmenopausal women — provides null evidence on a commonly recommended supplement.

> Short-term inorganic nitrate supplementation did not modify cardiovascular reactivity to handgrip exercise, post-exercise ischemia, or cold pressor test in healthy postmenopausal women. Findings do not support dietary nitrate as a strategy for reducing exaggerated blood pressure responses in this population.

### Ovariectomy Promotes Respiratory Instability and Risk of Obesity in Adult Female Rats
- **Authors:** Gagnon M et al.
- **Journal:** Experimental Physiology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42786687
- **DOI:** [10.1113/EP094077](https://doi.org/10.1113/EP094077)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT — ovarian hormone loss)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Mechanistic preclinical study demonstrating that ovarian hormone deficiency (not intermittent hypoxia alone) drives sleep apnoea pathophysiology — reinforces menopause as a primary risk window for respiratory problems and supports HRT as a potential modulator.

> OVX increased apnoea frequency, respiratory instability, and HPA axis activation, and augmented weight gain. Moderate intermittent hypoxia did not worsen these effects, underscoring ovarian hormone deficiency as the primary driver of sleep apnoea-related pathophysiology in menopause.
