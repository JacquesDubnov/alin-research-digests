### Pharmacologic Treatments for Females With Menopausal Vasomotor Symptoms: A Clinical Guideline From the American College of Physicians
- **Authors:** Qaseem A et al.
- **Journal:** Annals of Internal Medicine, 2026
- **PMID:** 42832802
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.7326/ANNALS-26-01205
- **Domain:** Query 3 — Non-hormonal & NK3 antagonists / VMS
- **Relevance to ALIN:** This is a landmark new guideline from the ACP that directly governs what clinicians will recommend for VMS; ALIN can use it to align treatment-option content and shared decision-making flows with the latest evidence-based standard of care.

> Guideline recommends estrogens (with or without progestogens) as first-line VMS treatment (high economic value, moderate–high certainty); neurokinin receptor antagonists (NKRAs), SNRIs, and SSRIs as alternatives; fezolinetant rated low economic value. Based on 90 randomized trials.

### Pharmacologic Treatments for Female Menopausal Vasomotor Symptoms: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis for the American College of Physicians
- **Authors:** Diem S et al.
- **Journal:** Annals of Internal Medicine, 2026
- **PMID:** 42832800
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.7326/ANNALS-26-00943
- **Domain:** Query 3 — Non-hormonal & NK3 antagonists / VMS
- **Relevance to ALIN:** The primary evidence base behind the ACP guideline above; confirms estrogens, NKRAs, SNRIs/SSRIs all improve VMS frequency and quality of life, with estrogens also reducing severity. Key for calibrating ALIN's symptom-treatment recommendation logic.

> 90 RCTs included. Estrogens reduced VMS frequency AND severity; NKRAs, SNRIs, SSRIs reduced frequency but not severity. All three classes improved menopause-related QoL and sleep. Few serious adverse events reported across all agents.

### Effects of Neurokinin Receptor Antagonists on Sleep Disturbance in Menopausal Women: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials
- **Authors:** Lemos MJ et al.
- **Journal:** Menopause, 2026
- **PMID:** 42837121
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000002903
- **Domain:** Query 3 — Non-hormonal & NK3 antagonists / VMS
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Directly supports the claim that fezolinetant and elinzanetant improve not only hot flashes but also sleep—a top user complaint in perimenopause. ALIN can highlight this non-hormonal sleep benefit when users are wary of HRT.

> 7 RCTs (3,184 women). NKRAs significantly improved PROMIS Sleep Disturbance scores at 4, 12, and 24 weeks vs. placebo; elinzanetant showed greater effect than fezolinetant. Evidence certainty low due to heterogeneity but trial sequential analysis confirmed sufficient information size.

### GLP-1 Receptor Agonists in Perimenopause and Early Postmenopause: Clinical Role, Cardiometabolic Benefits and Knowledge Gaps
- **Authors:** Ozcivit Erkan IB et al.
- **Journal:** Climacteric, 2026
- **PMID:** 42847987
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1080/13697137.2026.2735765
- **Domain:** Query 1 — HRT/MHT & menopause
- **Relevance to ALIN:** GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide, tirzepatide) are now widely used; this review addresses their role in the menopause transition specifically, including possible interactions with MHT—key for ALIN users managing weight and cardiometabolic risk simultaneously.

> GLP-1 RAs reduce visceral adiposity, improve glycemic control and cardiovascular outcomes in midlife women. Preliminary evidence suggests complementary effects with MHT. Long-term safety, effects on bone/muscle, and MHT interactions remain undefined—major research gap.

### From Black Box Warning to Wake-Up Call: Menopausal Hormone Therapy Prescribing Patterns at a Large Academic Medical Center
- **Authors:** Rusk KL et al.
- **Journal:** Menopause, 2026
- **PMID:** 42837132
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000002890
- **Domain:** Query 1 — HRT/MHT & menopause
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Confirms that MHT remains drastically underprescribed (7% of eligible women) with stark racial and insurance disparities—Black, Hispanic, and Asian women all had lower odds of receiving MHT. ALIN's health equity mission should be calibrated to these persistent gaps.

> Retrospective study of 305,053 women over 18 years. Only 6.99% had any MHT prescription. African American women had ~49% lower odds, Hispanic women ~36% lower odds than White women. Higher comorbidity burden and public insurance independently associated with lower prescribing.

### Awareness and Clinical Impact of the Revised FDA Warning on Menopausal Hormone Therapy Among Latin American Physicians. REDLINC XIV
- **Authors:** Blümel JE et al.
- **Journal:** Menopause, 2026
- **PMID:** 42837129
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000002893
- **Domain:** Query 1 — HRT/MHT & menopause
- **Relevance to ALIN:** The revised FDA "black box" warning on MHT is reshaping how clinicians talk about breast cancer risk globally; 90.5% of Latin American physicians believe the previous warning overstated risk. ALIN's content on MHT safety messaging can reference this evolving consensus.

> 2,166 physicians in 13 Latin American countries. 80.7% aware of revised FDA statement; 78.9% found it more balanced. Only 18.7% reported increased MHT prescribing despite attitude shift—gap between evidence acceptance and clinical behavior change.

### Interactions Between Vaginal, Periurethral and Urinary Microbiota in Genitourinary Syndrome of Menopause and the Effects of Local Estrogen Therapy
- **Authors:** Guler Cekic S et al.
- **Journal:** Maturitas, 2026
- **PMID:** 42858690
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.maturitas.2026.109146
- **Domain:** Query 5 — Vaginal estrogen / GSM / DHEA
- **Relevance to ALIN:** First study characterizing all three urogenital microbiome compartments in GSM; vaginal estriol therapy restored Lactobacillus dominance across vaginal and periurethral niches. Informs ALIN's content on why local estrogen treats both vaginal AND urinary symptoms.

> Prospective study: symptomatic women had reduced Lactobacillus and increased anaerobic taxa vaginally and periurethrally, with Escherichia-dominant urine. 12 weeks of vaginal estriol restored Lactobacillus dominance and shifted urinary microbiota toward a control-like profile.

### Use of Vaginal Estrogen in Breast and Gynecologic Cancer Survivors
- **Authors:** Burns JB et al.
- **Journal:** JCO Oncology Practice, 2026
- **PMID:** 42832731
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1200/OP-26-00625
- **Domain:** Query 5 — Vaginal estrogen / GSM / DHEA
- **Relevance to ALIN:** A significant proportion of ALIN users will be cancer survivors; this narrative review confirms low-dose vaginal estrogen is safe and effective in most, including ER-positive breast cancer survivors on tamoxifen. Critical for ALIN's safety disclaimers and shared decision-making content.

> Large observational studies and meta-analyses show no increased breast cancer recurrence or mortality with low-dose vaginal estrogen, including in ER-positive disease and tamoxifen users. Data for aromatase inhibitor users remain limited. Shared decision-making and multidisciplinary care recommended.

### Association of Statin Use With Severe Urogenital Symptoms and Lower Sexual Function in Postmenopausal Women: A Cross-Sectional Study
- **Authors:** Salinas C et al.
- **Journal:** Climacteric, 2026
- **PMID:** 42841261
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1080/13697137.2026.2735782
- **Domain:** Query 4/5 — Cardiovascular / GSM
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Statins are widely used by postmenopausal women for cardiovascular protection, but this study links statin use to worse GSM and sexual function—an underrecognized drug interaction that ALIN should flag as a comorbidity consideration.

> 1,184 postmenopausal women from 9 Latin American countries. Statin users had ~74% higher odds of severe urogenital symptoms and ~46% higher odds of lower sexual function after adjustment for confounders. Prospective confirmation needed.

### Associations of Psychosocial Supports and Stressors With Cognition in Midlife Women
- **Authors:** Oken E et al.
- **Journal:** Menopause, 2026
- **PMID:** 42837133
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000002888
- **Domain:** Query 4 — Cognitive / dementia
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Financial and food insecurity—not social isolation or perceived stress—independently predicted worse executive function and attention in midlife women; menopause status had negligible additional effect. ALIN's mental health module should address socioeconomic stressors as cognitive risk factors.

> 492 midlife women (mean age 55.5 y, 71% postmenopausal). Financial/food insecurity significantly associated with lower executive functioning, attention, and total cognition on CANTAB; social support, perceived stress, and discrimination were not independently associated after demographic adjustment.

### Early Menopause as a Female-Specific Prognostic Marker After Acute Coronary Syndrome in Women Aged 60 Years or Younger
- **Authors:** Marić Bešić K et al.
- **Journal:** Menopause, 2026
- **PMID:** 42837120
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000002896
- **Domain:** Query 4 — Cardiovascular
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Early menopause (before age 45) independently predicted worse outcomes after ACS, including ~78% higher hazard for MACE and ~2.5× higher mortality. ALIN should alert users with early menopause history to discuss cardiovascular surveillance with their physician.

> Multicenter registry, 728 women with ACS. Menopause before age 45 associated with HR 1.78 (MACE), HR 2.57 (death), HR 1.87 (composite), after adjustment. Associations attenuated with inclusion of surgical menopause and hormone therapy variables—supports causality investigation.

### Reproductive Characteristics, Postmenopausal Obesity, and Cardiovascular Disease Incidence in the Women's Health Initiative
- **Authors:** Borrowman JD et al.
- **Journal:** Journal of the American Heart Association, 2026
- **PMID:** 42786622
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1161/JAHA.126.049882
- **Domain:** Query 4 — Cardiovascular
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Large WHI analysis (119,182 women, median 12.8y follow-up) confirming early menarche, early menopause, and short reproductive lifespan all independently predict incident CVD; waist circumference mediates these associations. ALIN can use this to explain why managing abdominal weight gain during menopause matters for heart health.

> Early menopause (HR 1.22), early menarche (HR 1.07), and short reproductive lifespan (HR 1.21) each independently associated with CVD incidence. Waist circumference was a significant mediator—underscores visceral adiposity as a modifiable target.

- **Authors:** Aktoz F et al.
- **Journal:** Climacteric, 2026
- **PMID:** 42852718
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1080/13697137.2026.2735783
- **Domain:** Query 1 — HRT/MHT & menopause
- **Relevance to ALIN:** An underexplored domain—menopause affects lung function through declining sex hormones, contributing to asthma and COPD risk; MHT effects on pulmonary outcomes remain unclear. Expands ALIN's coverage beyond the "classic" menopause symptoms.
