### Reassessing the Neoplasm Signal with Fezolinetant: Regulatory Inference, Mechanistic Plausibility, and the Case for Proactive Pharmacovigilance
- **Authors:** Clack G et al.
- **Journal:** Drug Safety, 2026
- **PMID:** 42873405
- **DOI:** [10.1007/s40264-026-01737-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40264-026-01737-8)
- **Domain:** Query 3 (non-hormonal / NK3 antagonist)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Directly concerns fezolinetant safety — the leading non-hormonal vasomotor treatment ALIN discusses; regulators have so far dismissed the neoplasm imbalance, but this paper challenges that position with new mechanistic arguments.

> Exposure-adjusted incidence of non-benign neoplasms rose dose-dependently across placebo, 30 mg and 45 mg fezolinetant (0.2, 0.7, and 1.2 per 100 subject-years; RR 6.6 at licensed dose, 95% CI 0.9–51.3). Authors argue the regulatory literature review conflated NK3R with NK1R pharmacology and propose four plausible mechanisms (KISS1 suppression, NK1R compensation, anti-angiogenic loss, lesion acceleration). They call for structured post-authorisation surveillance and periodic independent safety review.

### Quantitative Assessment of Neurokinin Receptor Antagonists in Menopausal Hot Flashes: A Model-Based Meta-Analysis
- **Authors:** Wang S et al.
- **Journal:** Menopause, 2026
- **PMID:** 42868052
- **DOI:** [10.1097/GME.0000000000002909](https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000002909)
- **Domain:** Query 3 (non-hormonal / NK3 antagonist)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Provides the first pharmacodynamic model comparing fezolinetant and elinzanetant efficacy head-to-head and against estradiol and paroxetine — directly informative for ALIN's treatment-options content.

> At recommended doses, fezolinetant 45 mg and elinzanetant 120 mg achieved comparable reductions in hot flash frequency (20.6% and 19.6%, respectively) — roughly 52% and 49% of the effect seen with estradiol at 12 weeks, and greater than paroxetine (14.8%). Both show dose-dependent responses with rapid onset.

### Elinzanetant in Postmenopausal Women with Sleep Disturbance: Results from the Randomized, Phase II NIRVANA Study
- **Authors:** Baker FC et al.
- **Journal:** Sleep, 2026
- **PMID:** 42520241
- **DOI:** [10.1093/sleep/zsag208](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsag208)
- **Domain:** Query 3 (non-hormonal / NK3 antagonist)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Sleep disturbance is among the highest-reported perimenopause complaints; this Phase II RCT directly tests elinzanetant's effect on objective and subjective sleep in women with VMS.

> Elinzanetant reduced polysomnography wake-after-sleep-onset (WASO) by ~22.5 min versus placebo at Week 4; the effect attenuated by Week 12. Post-hoc random-coefficient modeling showed consistent benefits in WASO, total sleep time, sleep efficiency, and arousal index over 12 weeks across PSG, wearable (Sleepiz), and sleep diary measures.

### Menopausal Hormone Therapy Combined with Antiresorptive Therapy for Skeletal Outcomes: A Systematic Review and Network Meta-Analysis of RCTs
- **Authors:** Wang X et al.
- **Journal:** Menopause, 2026
- **PMID:** 42868070
- **DOI:** [10.1097/GME.0000000000002924](https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000002924)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Bone health is a core perimenopause concern; this network meta-analysis of 16 RCTs (n=2,861) gives the clearest comparative picture yet of combination MHT + bisphosphonate therapy for osteoporosis prevention.

> MHT + bisphosphonate showed the most favourable hip BMD profile (femoral neck MD +4.87%, femoral trochanter MD +6.65% vs. control) and was associated with lower fracture risk (RR 0.51, 95% CI 0.28–0.93), though fracture data were sparse. MHT alone also outperformed control across all skeletal sites.

### Trends of Menopausal Hormone Therapy Across Canadian Provinces in 2018–2023
- **Authors:** Farahvash A et al.
- **Journal:** Menopause, 2026
- **PMID:** 42868055
- **DOI:** [10.1097/GME.0000000000002925](https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000002925)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Population-level utilisation data show MHT use is rising but remains low and highly variable, reinforcing the gap ALIN aims to address for Canadian users specifically.

> National MHT use rose from 59.1 to 83.4 DDD/1000 women/day (2018–2023). Growth was driven by vaginal and transdermal formulations; oral MHT stayed flat. Quebec's use (162 DDD) far exceeded other provinces, likely tied to drug coverage differences.

### The Effectiveness of Physical Activity Interventions on Non-Physiological Symptoms of Menopause: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
- **Authors:** Saidel S et al.
- **Journal:** Menopause, 2026
- **PMID:** 42868040
- **DOI:** [10.1097/GME.0000000000002827](https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000002827)
- **Domain:** Query 2 (perimenopause / non-pharmacological)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Provides strong RCT-level evidence for physical activity as a depression and QoL intervention during the menopause transition — directly applicable to ALIN's lifestyle coaching content.

> 12 RCTs (n=1,003 women): physical activity significantly improved depression (g=−1.37), mood (g=0.84), psychological QoL (g=0.66), and overall QoL (g=1.10). Both aerobic and mind-body interventions were beneficial; effects present in both perimenopause and early postmenopause.

### Reclaiming Agency: How Women Build Mood, Confidence and Social Connections During Perimenopause
- **Authors:** Kumar S et al.
- **Journal:** Climacteric, 2026
- **PMID:** 42862120
- **DOI:** [10.1080/13697137.2026.2736441](https://doi.org/10.1080/13697137.2026.2736441)
- **Domain:** Query 2 (perimenopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Qualitative study of 30 culturally diverse Australian women mapping the lived experience of brain fog, mood, confidence, and social connectedness during perimenopause — rich material for ALIN's empathetic UX design and personalization.

> Perimenopause emerged as a recurring disruption-stabilisation cycle rather than linear decline. Women actively engineered stability via pacing/withdrawal, sleep optimisation, physical activity, organisational tools, reframing, dietary adjustment, and social support. Validated peer connection was especially valued.

### Menopausal Symptom Severities and Patterns Are Affected by Medical Profiles
- **Authors:** Andrews R et al.
- **Journal:** Post Reproductive Health, 2026
- **PMID:** 42872934
- **DOI:** [10.1080/20533691.2026.2741118](https://doi.org/10.1080/20533691.2026.2741118)
- **Domain:** Query 2 (perimenopause / comorbidities)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Largest real-world dataset to date (n=210,489 app users) linking specific comorbidities to menopausal symptom clusters — directly applicable to ALIN's personalised symptom tracking and AI-driven recommendations.

> Depression and anxiety showed the strongest symptom amplification. Bilateral oophorectomy increased vasomotor symptoms; POI and endometriosis drove vaginal symptoms; autoimmune disease drove joint aches. PCOS/adenomyosis/PMDD were paradoxically associated with lower vasomotor burden. Cancer treatment increased vaginal and vasomotor symptoms while reducing other domains.

### C-Reactive Protein–Triglyceride Glucose Index and All-Cause Mortality in Postmenopausal Women: A Dual-Cohort Study (NHANES and CHARLS)
- **Authors:** Huang R et al.
- **Journal:** International Journal of Gynaecology and Obstetrics, 2026
- **PMID:** 42861770
- **DOI:** [10.1002/ijgo.71373](https://doi.org/10.1002/ijgo.71373)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cardiovascular / cardiometabolic)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Validates a combined insulin-resistance/inflammation index (CTI) as a mortality predictor in postmenopausal women across two large multinational cohorts; relevant to ALIN's metabolic health tracking features.

### Lifestyle and Genetic Risk Factors for Postmenopausal Breast Cancer in the Multiethnic Cohort
- **Authors:** Zhao C et al.

- **PMID:** 42879705
- **DOI:** [10.1093/jnci/djag394](https://doi.org/10.1093/jnci/djag394)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (breast cancer)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Confirms that a healthy lifestyle reduces postmenopausal breast cancer risk by ~33% regardless of genetic susceptibility — empowering message for ALIN users concerned about cancer risk with MHT.

### Gynecologist and Menopausal Patient Perspectives on Vaginal Hormone Therapy for the Management of GSM in Canada: A Real-World Survey
- **Authors:** Shea AK et al.
- **Journal:** Patient Preference and Adherence, 2026
- **PMID:** 42890743
- **DOI:** [10.2147/PPA.S636147](https://doi.org/10.2147/PPA.S636147)
- **Domain:** Query 5 (vaginal estrogen / genitourinary syndrome of menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Real-world Canadian data on GSM treatment patterns, satisfaction, and gaps — directly relevant to ALIN's localisation for Canadian users and content on vaginal estrogen/DHEA.

> Among 119 GSM patients (naïve and on-treatment cohorts): 77% on vaginal HT were satisfied but 77% would consider switching (mainly for convenience). Ease of use, onset of action, and low systemic absorption were top treatment selection factors.
