### Reappraising the Safety of Menopausal Hormone Therapy for Women — Implications for the Clinic
- **Authors:** Hirschberg AL et al.
- **Journal:** Journal of Clinical Endocrinology and Metabolism, 2026
- **PMID:** 42924623
- **DOI:** [10.1210/clinem/dgag404](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgag404)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT/menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Comprehensive safety narrative directly relevant to ALIN's core HRT guidance content; synthesises timing hypothesis, formulation differences, and individualized prescribing in one accessible review.

> For healthy women initiating MHT before age 60 or within 10 years of menopause, the overall benefit–risk profile is favourable. Transdermal estradiol is associated with lower VTE risk than oral; micronized progesterone and dydrogesterone show a more favourable breast-safety profile than some synthetic progestins.

### Menopause and Neurology: Clinical Essentials
- **Authors:** Thornton HF, Bove R, Moores G, Pikula A, Bui E et al.
- **Journal:** Neurology: Clinical Practice, 2026
- **PMID:** 42913453
- **DOI:** [10.1212/CPJ.0000000000200678](https://doi.org/10.1212/CPJ.0000000000200678)
- **Domain:** Queries 2, 4 (perimenopause; cardiovascular/cognitive)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Consolidates evidence on cognition, epilepsy, migraine, MS, and stroke across the menopause transition; confirms transdermal estradiol preference for stroke risk, with timing caveats useful for ALIN safety messaging.

> Perimenopause is often associated with transient subjective cognitive complaints partly attributed to hypoestrogenism. Oral estrogen-containing MHT is associated with increased thromboembolic events including stroke; transdermal regimens appear safer and are preferred at low or standard doses.

### Cardiovascular and Thromboembolic Risks of Sex Hormone Therapy: From Contraception to Transgender Care
- **Authors:** Bonnet F, Vaduva P, Dosda A et al.
- **Journal:** Annales d'Endocrinologie, 2026
- **PMID:** 42918571
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.ando.2026.103115](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ando.2026.103115)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cardiovascular/VTE), Query 5 (testosterone)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Comprehensive review integrating VTE and cardiovascular risk across the spectrum of sex hormone therapies; transdermal estradiol with micronized progesterone appearing safest for menopause — directly applicable to ALIN's HRT safety guidance.

> For menopause hormone therapy, route of administration is the main determinant of VTE risk: transdermal estradiol with micronized progesterone appears safest. No combined pill is acceptable after a VTE event or with a thrombogenic mutation; transdermal estradiol should be preferred for menopause.

### Change in Alcohol Consumption and All-Cause Mortality Among Postmenopausal Women Diagnosed with Breast Cancer
- **Authors:** Rees S, Bodelon C, Freudenheim JL et al.
- **Journal:** Breast Cancer Research and Treatment, 2026
- **PMID:** 42909681
- **DOI:** [10.1007/s10549-026-08113-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10549-026-08113-1)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (postmenopausal/breast cancer)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Nuanced longitudinal data on alcohol and survival post-breast cancer in postmenopausal women; relevant to ALIN's lifestyle guidance for breast cancer survivors considering or on HRT.

> Among women drinking ≥1 drink/day pre-diagnosis, increasing consumption was associated with higher all-cause mortality (HR 1.83). Alcohol reduction or cessation at pre-diagnosis was not associated with decreased mortality. Cessation occurring pre- to post-diagnosis was associated with increased ACM (HR 1.39), possibly reflecting reverse causation.

- **Journal:** Canadian Journal of Diabetes, 2026
- **PMID:** 42901271
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.jcjd.2026.10.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcjd.2026.10.002)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cardiovascular)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Preclinical mechanistic evidence that loss of ovarian hormones accelerates diabetic cardiomyopathy — supports ALIN's messaging about why menopausal transition increases cardiovascular risk in women with T2D.

> Only ovariectomised (not sham) female mice with T2D developed diastolic dysfunction and cardiomyocyte hypertrophy. OVX mice with T2D also had more pronounced reductions in cardiac glucose oxidation. Ovarian hormones appear to be key mediators of cardiac protection in females with diabetes.
