### Consensus Guidance for Female-Specific Prevention and Management of Cardiovascular Disease
- **Authors:** Khan SS et al. (AHA, AAP, ACOG, SMFM multi-society)
- **Journal:** Circulation, 2026
- **PMID:** 42929102
- **DOI:** [10.1161/CIR.0000000000001482](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001482)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cardiovascular/menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Landmark multi-society scientific statement defining four Female Life Stages (perimenarche, ovarian cycling, perimenopause, postmenopause) as a framework for cardiovascular screening and prevention — directly maps to how ALIN should frame risk conversations across the menopausal transition.

> Integrates hormonal, psychosocial, and cardiovascular health domains across the life course; provides consensus-based clinical guidance for each Female Life Stage including perimenopause (Stage 3) and postmenopause (Stage 4). Crosswalks major society guidelines and identifies implementation gaps.

### Safety and Tolerability of Elinzanetant: A Pooled Analysis from Four Randomized Clinical Trials
- **Authors:** Pinkerton JV, Francuski M, Nappi RE et al.
- **Journal:** EClinicalMedicine, 2026
- **PMID:** 42923369
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.eclinm.2026.104250](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2026.104250)
- **Domain:** Query 3 (NK3 antagonist/non-hormonal)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Comprehensive pooled safety data (n=1,113 women, 52 weeks) for elinzanetant, an approved dual NK1/NK3 receptor antagonist for vasomotor symptoms. No liver toxicity signal, no endometrial hyperplasia. Critical for ALIN's non-hormonal treatment information.

> Most common adverse events: headache, fatigue, somnolence. Discontinuation rate 7.8% vs. 3.6% placebo over 12 weeks; no Hy's Law cases or cholestatic injury; no endometrial hyperplasia or malignancy in either group. Overall favorable safety profile confirmed across SWITCH-1 and OASIS 1-3 trials.

### Cost-Effectiveness Analysis of Menopausal Hormone Therapy
- **Authors:** Gill E, Zeng W, Shvartsman K, Brown J
- **Journal:** Obstetrics and Gynecology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42594384
- **DOI:** [10.1097/AOG.0000000000006404](https://doi.org/10.1097/AOG.0000000000006404)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Markov model showing MHT is cost-saving AND gains QALYs — "absolute dominance" over no MHT. Cardiovascular risk reduction is the biggest driver. Strongly supports ALIN messaging on MHT value for appropriate candidates.

> Per 10,000 women over lifetime, transdermal E2 vs. no MHT: $135M less expensive, 33,196 QALYs gained, 259 fewer ASCVD events, 87 more breast cancers, 181 fewer hip fractures, 647 fewer deaths. MHT with micronized progesterone similarly dominant. ASCVD reduction is the largest cost-effectiveness driver.

### Anti-Müllerian Hormone Trajectories Across the Menopause Transition
- **Authors:** Acharya KS, Bozzuto L, Kaizer LK et al. (SWAN)
- **Journal:** Obstetrics and Gynecology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42945500
- **DOI:** [10.1097/AOG.0000000000006451](https://doi.org/10.1097/AOG.0000000000006451)
- **Domain:** Query 2 (perimenopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** SWAN data showing two distinct AMH decline patterns — "low/slow" vs. "high/fast" — with the low/slow group having worse postmenopausal cardiovascular risk profiles and fewer vasomotor symptoms. Informs ALIN's explanation of why menopause transition looks different for different women.

> 39.6% of women had lower initial AMH and slower decline (low/slow group) — this group had worse postmenopausal cardiovascular risk independent of premenopausal risk factors, yet reported fewer vasomotor symptoms after the final menstrual period. AMH trajectory may be a novel cardiovascular risk marker.

### When a Psychiatric Diagnosis Is Actually Perimenopause
- **Authors:** Ramey ES
- **Journal:** American Journal of Nursing, 2026
- **PMID:** 42814536
- **DOI:** [10.1097/AJN.0000000000000393](https://doi.org/10.1097/AJN.0000000000000393)
- **Domain:** Query 2 (perimenopause/symptoms)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Directly addresses a core ALIN use case — perimenopausal women commonly misdiagnosed with primary psychiatric disorders. Challenges five clinical misconceptions and provides nurses/clinicians with tools to recognise the pattern. Reinforces ALIN's role in helping users connect hormonal changes with mental health symptoms.

> Perimenopause neurobiological effects (serotonin, dopamine, GABA, HPA axis disruption) produce a symptom profile nearly identical to primary psychiatric disorders. Diagnosis does not require abnormal labs or menstrual irregularity. A hormonally-informed psychiatric assessment framework is presented.

### Effectiveness of Digital Interventions for Menopausal Symptoms and Cardiometabolic Outcomes: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
- **Authors:** Goh SPC, Chen RJ, Li SWL, Chew HSJ
- **Journal:** Maturitas, 2026
- **PMID:** 42822207
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.maturitas.2026.109136](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.maturitas.2026.109136)
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cardiovascular/menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Meta-analysis of 18 RCTs directly quantifying the effectiveness of digital health interventions for menopausal symptoms — the evidence base for ALIN's category of intervention.

> Digital interventions significantly reduced hot-flash frequency (SMD -0.30), improved sleep quality (MD -3.38 points) and reduced waist circumference (MD -2.23 cm). No significant pooled effects on night sweats, blood pressure, lipids or glycaemia. Evidence certainty: moderate for hot flashes, very low for sleep. Calls for longer interventions in well-defined menopausal populations.

### The Impact of Midlife Hormonal Treatment on Lower Urinary Tract Symptoms
- **Authors:** Byrne EH, Von Bargen EC
- **Journal:** Current Opinion in Obstetrics & Gynecology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42952301
- **DOI:** [10.1097/GCO.0000000000001159](https://doi.org/10.1097/GCO.0000000000001159)
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT) & Query 5 (vaginal estrogen)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Clarifies the divergent effects of MHT route on urinary symptoms — vaginal estrogen helps LUTS, systemic estrogen can worsen them. Key for ALIN's genitourinary symptom guidance.

> Vaginal estrogen reliably improves lower urinary tract symptoms; periurethral application non-inferior to intravaginal. Systemic estrogen is associated with new and worsening LUTS. SERMs appear less deleterious than systemic MHT for LUTS. Mechanism of divergent effects remains unclear.

### Menopausal Symptoms, Sexual Function, and Psychological Well-Being in Women Across the Menopausal Transition
- **Authors:** Brenk-Franz K, Petersen M, Strauß B
- **Journal:** Comprehensive Psychoneuroendocrinology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42920980
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.cpnec.2026.100387](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpnec.2026.100387)
- **Domain:** Query 2 (perimenopause/symptoms)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Cross-sectional study (n=539) showing 63.4% of women across all menopausal stages score below the sexual dysfunction threshold; perimenopause shows the worst psychological well-being. Urogenital symptoms are the strongest predictor of sexual dysfunction.

> Mean FSFI scores fell below the screening cut-off across all menopausal stages. Perimenopausal women had highest symptom burden and lowest well-being. Urogenital symptom burden was the strongest multivariate predictor of sexual function across all FSFI domains — more influential than menopausal stage per se.

### Associations of Loneliness and Social Isolation with Menopausal Symptom Burden: CLIMATERE Cohort
- **Authors:** Vallée A, Horsman S, Ceccaldi PF et al.
- **Journal:** Maturitas, 2026
- **PMID:** 42805125
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.maturitas.2026.109135](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.maturitas.2026.109135)
- **Domain:** Query 2 (perimenopause/symptoms)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Population-based cohort (n=4,124) demonstrating that loneliness independently predicts greater menopausal symptom burden. Directly relevant to ALIN's psychosocial support features and the social isolation problem in perimenopause.

> Loneliness alone: B=2.91 higher Greene Climacteric Scale score, OR=1.77 for higher symptom quartile, OR=1.68 for severe symptoms. Social isolation showed inconsistent effects dependent on threshold definition. Loneliness is consistently and independently associated with greater menopausal symptom burden.

### Interactions Between Vaginal, Periurethral and Urinary Microbiota in GSM and Effects of Local Estrogen Therapy
- **Authors:** Guler Cekic S, Vatansever C, Akpinar A et al.
- **Journal:** Maturitas, 2026
- **PMID:** 42858690
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.maturitas.2026.109146](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.maturitas.2026.109146)
- **Domain:** Query 5 (vaginal estrogen/GSM)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** First study to characterize all three urogenital microbiome compartments (vaginal, periurethral, urinary) in postmenopausal women and show local estrogen restores Lactobacillus dominance across all niches. Supports ALIN's explanation of why GSM affects urinary health.

> Local estrogen therapy restored Lactobacillus-dominant communities in vaginal and periurethral compartments and shifted urinary microbiota toward a control-like composition. Periurethral Streptococcus abundance correlated with sexual symptom scores. Microbial alterations extend beyond the vagina and are compartment-specific.

### Use of Vaginal Estrogens in Urogynecology: Current Evidence, Controversies and Clinical Applications
- **Authors:** Singh P, Pancholy J, Pancholy A
- **Journal:** Current Opinion in Obstetrics & Gynecology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42893492
- **DOI:** [10.1097/GCO.0000000000001157](https://doi.org/10.1097/GCO.0000000000001157)
- **Domain:** Query 5 (vaginal estrogen/GSM)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Comprehensive review updating evidence on vaginal estrogen: confirms safety (no CV, VTE, or malignancy risk), supports its use for recurrent UTIs, clarifies evolving use in breast cancer survivors, and notes it does NOT improve anatomic surgical outcomes for prolapse.

> Low-dose vaginal estrogen significantly improves GSM symptoms and restores epithelial integrity. Large cohort studies show no increased CVD, VTE, or malignancy risk. Breast cancer survivors on aromatase inhibitors warrant caution. New 2025 multisociety guideline recommends topical (not systemic) estrogen for recurrent UTI prevention.

### Bipolar Disorder and Perimenopause: An Update (Systematic Review)
- **Authors:** Radosavljevic J, Bengi D, Frey BN
- **Journal:** Bipolar Disorders, 2026
- **PMID:** 42811911
- **DOI:** [10.1111/bdi.70186](https://doi.org/10.1111/bdi.70186)
- **Domain:** Query 2 (perimenopause/treatment)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Systematic review (14 studies) showing perimenopause is a period of increased risk for first-onset bipolar disorder AND worsened illness course in existing BD. Highlights the gap in evidence for HRT in this group. Important for ALIN's mental health screening and referral pathways.

> Converging evidence that perimenopause increases risk of first-onset bipolar disorder. In existing BD: increased depression and heightened anxiety are the most consistent findings during perimenopause; hypo/mania findings were mixed. Evidence on HRT in this population is "limited or absent." Standardised menopause staging (STRAW+10) recommended for future research.
