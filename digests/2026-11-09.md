### Reproductive Lifespan and Hormonal Therapy in Relation to Later-Life Neurovascular Health in Community-Dwelling Older Women
- **Authors:** Lorenzon G et al.
- **Journal:** Neurology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42981558
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218706
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT) & Query 4 (cardiovascular/cognitive)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Directly supports ALIN's messaging around MHT and brain health — longer estrogen exposure (both endogenous and via MHT) linked to lower cerebrovascular disease burden and, among APOE-ε4 carriers, greater cortical thickness in Alzheimer's-vulnerable regions.

> Among 352 women (mean age 70), longer reproductive span was associated with lower cerebral small vessel disease burden (OR=0.92). MHT use was associated with fewer perivascular spaces and microbleeds, greater white matter integrity, and — in APOE-ε4 carriers specifically — greater AD-related cortical thickness. Combined OC + MHT use showed highest fractional anisotropy vs non-users (β=0.45).

### Hormone Replacement Therapy and Cerebral Hemodynamics in Postmenopausal Women: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
- **Authors:** Vetter E et al.
- **Journal:** Journal of Applied Physiology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42997989
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.90127.2026
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Provides mechanistic evidence for how HRT protects cerebral circulation — reduced pulsatility index (a marker of arterial stiffness) — supporting ALIN's brain health content. Estrogen-only regimens showed strongest effects.

> Meta-analysis of 38 studies: HRT associated with significantly lower cerebrovascular pulsatility index vs controls (pooled MD −0.051, 95% CI −0.089 to −0.012). Within-individual analyses confirmed reduction over time (p=0.004). Estrogen-only regimens produced greater reductions than combined or tibolone. Route of administration showed no consistent differential effect.

### Perimenopause and Cardiometabolic Risk: Emerging Evidence, Therapeutic Opportunities and Unanswered Questions
- **Authors:** El Khoudary SR et al.
- **Journal:** Climacteric, 2026
- **PMID:** 42969289
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1080/13697137.2026.2745378
- **Domain:** Query 2 (perimenopause) & Query 4 (cardiovascular)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** High-priority review for ALIN's cardiovascular content — perimenopause is framed as a "physiologic convergence point" where vasomotor symptoms, sleep disturbance, and cardiometabolic risk intersect; calls for interventional trials specifically during perimenopause.

> Adverse lipid, insulin, and vascular changes during perimenopause exceed aging-alone expectations. Vasomotor symptoms and sleep disturbance are associated with worse cardiometabolic profiles. Prospective data suggest poor cardiovascular health predicts worse symptom experience, implying bidirectional relationship. No RCT evidence yet exists specifically for the perimenopausal window.

### From Cancer Prevention to Survivorship: Comprehensive Menopause Management After Risk-Reducing Salpingo-Oophorectomy
- **Authors:** da Silva Filho AL et al.
- **Journal:** International Journal of Gynaecology and Obstetrics, 2026
- **PMID:** 42998015
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ijgo.71486
- **Domain:** Query 1 (HRT/MHT) & Query 3 (non-hormonal)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Directly relevant to users who've had RRSO — covers both hormonal (MHT) and non-hormonal options (NK3 antagonists, antidepressants, CBT) for surgical menopause, with updated evidence on BRCA1/2 carriers.

> RRSO induces immediate surgical menopause. MHT is first-line for vasomotor symptoms and bone protection. Recent prospective data: estrogen-only MHT associated with lower breast cancer incidence in BRCA1/2 carriers; combined E+P not statistically associated with increased risk. Non-hormonal options for HR+ breast cancer survivors include fezolinetant/elinzanetant, SSRIs, gabapentin, and CBT.

### Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events Following Vaginal Estrogen Therapy 2004–2023
- **Authors:** Rustia G et al.
- **Journal:** Urogynecology, 2026
- **PMID:** 42981118
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1097/SPV.0000000000001949
- **Domain:** Query 5 (vaginal estrogen/GSM)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Directly addresses patient concerns about vaginal estrogen safety — large real-world dataset (301,701 women) showing vaginal estrogen is not associated with increased MACE hazard, countering FDA black-box warning fears.

> Retrospective cohort of 301,701 women with GSM. Vaginal estrogen cohort had lower odds of MACE (OR 0.87) and no significant difference in MACE hazard (HR 0.98, 95% CI 0.95–1.01) vs controls. Recent MACE history (OR 12.3) and obesity were strong predictors of subsequent MACE. Findings support safety of vaginal estrogen even in higher cardiovascular risk patients.

### Dehydroepiandrosterone in Menopausal Medicine: From Adrenal Aging to Intracrinology and Personalised Therapy
- **Authors:** Parra-Armendaris MM et al.
- **Journal:** Maturitas, 2026
- **PMID:** 42998568
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.maturitas.2026.109182
- **Domain:** Query 5 (DHEA/menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Comprehensive review of DHEA/prasterone relevant to ALIN's coverage of GSM treatments — distinguishes systemic oral DHEA (not endorsed by guidelines) from intravaginal prasterone (FDA/EMA approved for GSM/dyspareunia).

> After menopause, essentially all estrogens in peripheral tissues derive from intracrine conversion of adrenal DHEA. Adrenal DHEA declines from the third decade (adrenopause), creating dual hormonal deficit. Oral DHEA lacks convincing evidence for menopausal symptoms. Intravaginal prasterone (6.5 mg) improves GSM and dyspareunia while keeping serum sex steroids within normal postmenopausal range — FDA and EMA approved.

### Depressive Symptoms and Central Obesity Over the Menopausal Transition and the Development of Type 2 Diabetes Later in Life
- **Authors:** Chen Y et al. (SWAN cohort)
- **Journal:** Biopsychosocial Science and Medicine, 2026
- **PMID:** 42976091
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1097/PSY.0000000000001545
- **Domain:** Query 4 (cognitive/metabolic via menopause)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Highlights the mental-metabolic intersection during the menopausal transition — depressive symptoms during perimenopause predict T2D, partially mediated by central obesity, reinforcing ALIN's integrated approach to mental and physical health.

> SWAN cohort (n=1,719): cumulative depressive symptom burden during menopausal transition associated with higher odds of incident T2D postmenopause (OR=1.27 for somatic subscale). Central obesity mediated 21–25% of this association. Somatic depressive symptoms showed strongest effects, independent of demographic and metabolic covariates.

### Menopause, Weight Loss, and Pelvic Floor Changes: Clinical and Aesthetic Considerations

- **PMID:** 42975544
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1097/DSS.0000000000005435
- **Domain:** Query 5 (GSM/pelvic floor)
- **Relevance to ALIN:** Addresses GLP-1 agonist weight loss effects on pelvic floor in menopausal women — a highly timely topic given rise of ozempic/semaglutide use, with practical guidance for ALIN's users navigating both menopause and weight management.
